Relocating to Scottsdale? Here's What to Know

What out-of-state buyers should know about Scottsdale and 85250 before they land — from an agent who lives in Chaparral Park.

I help buyers relocate to Scottsdale from across the country, including some who move sight-unseen. As a Chaparral Park resident, I see the move from both sides: what newcomers worry about before they land, and what they're glad they chose once they've settled in. Here is what tends to matter most.

Plan Around the Climate

Summers here are genuinely hot, with temperatures regularly above 100 degrees from June through September, while winters are mild and sunny — the reason so many buyers arrive as seasonal visitors before making the move permanent. If you can, plan your house-hunting trip for the cooler months, when it's easiest to judge a neighborhood's outdoor lifestyle honestly. That matters in 85250, where much of daily life happens on the park lawns and the greenbelt path.

“Scottsdale” Isn't One Market

Newcomers often search for “Scottsdale homes” without realizing how different the sub-markets are:

  • Chaparral Park / 85250: central, walkable and park-side — a short walk or drive from Old Town Scottsdale and Scottsdale Fashion Square, in the Scottsdale Unified School District
  • North Scottsdale: golf-anchored, guard-gated and resort-adjacent
  • Arcadia & Arcadia Lite (Phoenix): historic ranch character, canal-and-mountain geography, destination dining
  • Central Phoenix: historic districts, urban proximity, architectural variety

Getting specific about lifestyle priorities early narrows the search dramatically. If walking to the park, the greenbelt and dinner sounds like you, read why Chaparral Park is the heart of 85250. If you're weighing other parts of the Valley, I cover those on my main site: Scottsdale, Arcadia, Arcadia Lite and Central Phoenix.

You Can Buy Remotely — With the Right Support

Out-of-state buyers regularly purchase homes here without an in-person visit before closing, relying on a local agent for video walkthroughs, honest neighborhood context and coordination through inspection and closing. It isn't the norm for every buyer, but it is a well-established path for the right situation. Because I live in the neighborhood, I can show you what's around a house — the park, the traffic, the sounds of a weekday evening — not just the house itself.

Factor in Commute and Airport Access

Central locations generally mean a shorter drive to Sky Harbor International Airport and to central business districts, while North Scottsdale's more remote communities mean a longer commute in both directions. If frequent travel or a daily commute is part of your routine, weigh that alongside the lifestyle you want.

Talk to Someone Who Knows the Details

HOA structures, school boundaries and what a specific block is really like all vary in this market. A conversation with a local agent before you start touring can save weeks of searching in the wrong neighborhoods. When you're ready, see how I work with buyers or contact me.

FAQ: Relocating to Scottsdale

What is the weather like in Scottsdale?

Scottsdale has a desert climate with hot summers, often exceeding 100 degrees Fahrenheit from June through September, and mild, sunny winters that draw seasonal residents from across the country. Many newcomers plan their move and house-hunting trips around the cooler months.

Can I buy a home in Scottsdale without visiting in person first?

Yes. Many out-of-state buyers purchase remotely with the help of a local agent who can provide video walkthroughs, detailed neighborhood context and guidance through inspection and closing, though an in-person visit before or shortly after closing is still recommended when possible.

Which Scottsdale neighborhood is right for someone relocating?

It depends on lifestyle priorities. Buyers wanting golf and resort-style living look toward North Scottsdale, while buyers wanting a central, walkable, park-side location often choose Chaparral Park and 85250. Those looking at Phoenix often consider Arcadia, Arcadia Lite or Central Phoenix's historic districts.

Is Chaparral Park a good area for people relocating to Scottsdale?

Many relocating buyers like Chaparral Park and 85250 because it is central and walkable, sits within the Scottsdale Unified School District, and is only minutes from Old Town Scottsdale and Scottsdale Fashion Square.
