What Not to Fix When Selling a House: Where Scottsdale Sellers Should (and Shouldn't) Spend Before Listing

Skip big remodels. 2025 Cost vs. Value data for the Mountain region shows exterior fixes return the most. What to fix, what to skip and what to disclose.

What shouldn't you fix when selling a house? Usually big remodels, additions and taste-driven upgrades. In the 2025 Cost vs. Value report's Mountain region, which includes Phoenix, an upscale major kitchen remodel recouped just 34.9% of its cost at resale, and an upscale bath remodel recouped 36.4%. Meanwhile garage door replacement returned 236.1% and a steel entry door 186.3%.

The bottom line for 85250 sellers: fix what's broken, unsafe or visible from the curb. Skip the renovation a buyer may redo anyway. Disclose what you know. Here's how to sort your list.

Data note: Project returns are Zonda's 2025 Cost vs. Value report (Journal of Light Construction), Mountain region figures. Market figures are the Phoenix REALTORS/ShowingTime Local Market Update for Scottsdale, October 2026. Contract terms are from the Phoenix REALTORS example Residential Resale Purchase Contract (Dec. 2024). Read November 10, 2026.

Cost Recouped at Resale, Mountain Region (2025)

Highest returns:

  • Garage door replacement: $4,968 job cost, 236.1% recouped
  • Entry door replacement (steel): $2,446, 186.3%
  • Manufactured stone veneer: $11,991, 161.8%
  • Minor kitchen remodel (midrange): $28,490, 110.3%

Lowest returns:

  • Major kitchen remodel (upscale): $159,439, 34.9%
  • Bath remodel (upscale): $79,492, 36.4%
  • Backyard patio: $52,218, 38.3%
  • Solar power installation: $51,183, 28.4%
  • Primary suite addition (upscale): $350,045, 15.6%

Zonda's summary: 8 of the top 10 projects nationally were exterior replacements, and a minor kitchen remodel was the only interior project in the top five.

What Not to Fix

Full kitchen or bath remodels

A buyer paying Scottsdale prices often has their own vision. Above, upscale versions recouped about a third of their cost. Deep cleaning, fresh caulk, new cabinet hardware or updated lighting usually present better for far less.

Additions and big outdoor projects

A new primary suite, patio or pool remodel takes months and returned well under half its cost in the Mountain data.

Cosmetic wear buyers expect

  • Minor driveway cracks
  • Older but working appliances
  • Dated (not damaged) light fixtures
  • Small nail holes and scuffs beyond a basic touch-up

Code upgrades on a working system

Older homes often aren't built to today's code. If something works and is safe, discuss with your agent whether to disclose and price it in rather than rebuild it.

What to Fix

  • Safety items: broken stair rails, trip hazards, pool barrier problems
  • Active leaks and water damage, plus the source
  • Anything likely to trip up a buyer's loan: ask your agent which condition issues lenders and appraisers in your price range tend to flag
  • Curb appeal: garage door, front door, paint touch-ups, desert landscaping cleanup
  • Small, cheap repairs a buyer would otherwise list: dripping faucets, broken screens, dead bulbs

A pre-listing inspection helps you decide with facts instead of guesses.

Arizona's Rules: Disclose, Don't Hide

The Phoenix REALTORS contract says sellers must disclose known material facts and defects, including known insurance claims in the past 5 years. It also says the seller does not have an obligation to repair, correct or cure known defects that are disclosed. See our Arizona seller disclosure (SPDS) guide.

During the inspection period (the form's residential option is 10 days), the buyer may deliver one signed notice of objections. The seller has 5 days to respond in writing unless the parties agree otherwise. That is where most repair decisions get made.

Why This Matters in Scottsdale Right Now

Phoenix REALTORS' October 2026 update for Scottsdale single-family homes shows a market that rewards value:

  • Median sales price: $1,225,000, up 5.6% year over year
  • Days on market until sale: 75
  • Percent of list price received: 96.8%
  • Months supply of inventory: 4.2

With buyers taking their time and negotiating, a clean, well-maintained home priced right tends to beat an over-improved one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should you not fix when selling a house?

Major remodels, additions and taste-driven upgrades. Upscale kitchens recouped 34.9% in the Mountain region.

What repairs give the best return before selling?

Exterior items: garage door (236.1%), steel entry door (186.3%) and stone veneer (161.8%).

Do I have to fix things before selling a house in Arizona?

Not under the standard contract, but you must disclose known material defects.

Should I fix things the buyer's inspector finds?

Negotiate: repairs, credits or price changes, in response to the buyer's single objection notice.
