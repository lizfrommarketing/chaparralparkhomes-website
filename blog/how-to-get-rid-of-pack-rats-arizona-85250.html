How to Get Rid of Pack Rats in Arizona: Keeping Them Out of Your 85250 Yard, Attic and Car

How to get rid of pack rats in Arizona: seal entry points, remove what attracts them, protect car wiring and clean nests safely. University of Arizona tips for 85250.

How do you get rid of pack rats in Arizona? Start with exclusion: seal the gaps they use to get into attics, walls and garages, then remove what attracts them, including fallen fruit, yard debris, leaky irrigation and dense nesting cover. University of Arizona Cooperative Extension calls pest-proofing the best way to keep rodents out. Trapping alone is not a long-term solution.

Why bother? Pack rats chew wiring in cars and appliances, and their nests can host kissing bugs and disease. In 85250, with mature citrus, desert plantings and plenty of garages, a little prevention goes a long way.

Data note: Biology and management guidance come from University of Arizona Cooperative Extension publications (2023 School & Home IPM "Pack rats," Backyard Gardener #23 "Woodrats," and AZ1677 on pest-proofing). General information, not pest control or medical advice.

How to Recognize a Pack Rat

Pack rats, also called wood rats or trade rats, belong to the genus Neotoma. Key facts from UA Extension:

  • Size: 12-14 inches including the tail; the body alone is 6-7 inches
  • Look: soft fur, a furred tail, and large eyes and ears
  • Habits: mostly nocturnal, strong diggers and climbers
  • Collectors: drawn to small, shiny objects like coins and jewelry, often leaving sticks or nuts in "trade"
  • Breeding: usually early summer, with up to 5 litters a year of 4-5 young
  • Lifespan: about 1-2 years

Signs You Have Them

  • Middens (nests): piles of sticks, cactus pieces and debris; ground dens can reach 3 to 5 feet high and across
  • Nests at the base of cholla or prickly pear, under shrubs or tucked against a wall
  • Chewed wires under the hood or in the garage
  • Nipped flowers or missing fruit from the garden

What Attracts Pack Rats to an 85250 Yard

UA Extension's guidance focuses on food, water and shelter:

  • Fallen fruit, especially from backyard citrus. See our guide on when to pick oranges in Arizona
  • Leaves, twigs and debris piled along walls
  • Leaking irrigation lines
  • Dense cactus and shrub cover right next to the house
  • Seldom-driven cars parked in one spot for long stretches

How to Keep Pack Rats Out

1. Seal the House and Garage

UA Extension's checklist:

  • Seal cracks and gaps around doors and windows
  • Close openings along foundations and under eaves or roofs
  • Seal gaps around utility line entry points
  • Remember the rule of thumb: if a rodent can fit its head through an opening, it may fit its body, and it may chew soft material to widen a hole

2. Clean Up the Yard

  • Pick up fallen fruit and clear debris
  • Fix irrigation leaks promptly
  • Inspect chollas and cacti regularly for new nests
  • Use smooth sheet-metal barriers at least two feet tall to protect individual plants or garden beds

3. Protect Your Car

Pack rats nest in the engine compartments of seldom-driven cars and are notorious for chewing wiring. Drive vehicles regularly, park in a closed garage when you can, and keep the area around parking spots clear.

Trapping, Legal Notes and Safe Cleanup

  • Trapping: UA Extension says lethal trapping may be warranted where significant damage occurs. Relocating live-trapped animals is not recommended, since released animals often die from exposure, predation or competition.
  • Legal status: UA's 2023 IPM guide notes native rodents are classified as nongame mammals protected by Arizona state law, with control allowed in some cases of significant property damage or disease risk. When in doubt, check with Arizona Game and Fish or a licensed pest control company.
  • Health: nests are common breeding sites for kissing bugs, white-throated pack rats are reported carriers of arenaviruses, and hantavirus can be present in nests.
  • Cleanup: soak a nest with disinfectant before disturbing it, wear disposable gloves, never handle rodents bare-handed, and follow the CDC's rodent cleanup steps.

Buying a Home? Look for Clues

During your inspection period, ask the inspector to note rodent activity in the attic, garage and around utility penetrations. For other desert pests, see our guides to scorpions in Scottsdale and termites in Arizona.

FAQ: Pack Rats in Arizona

What attracts pack rats to a yard?

Fallen fruit, debris, leaky irrigation and dense cactus cover.

How do I keep pack rats out of my car engine?

Drive it regularly, park in a closed garage and clear nesting cover.

Do pack rats in Arizona carry diseases?

They can. Kissing bugs, arenaviruses and hantavirus are concerns.

What is the best way to get rid of pack rats?

Exclusion plus removing food and shelter. Trapping alone won't solve it.
