How Long Does a Roof Last in Arizona? Tile, Foam & Shingle Roofs in 85250

How long does a roof last in Arizona? Tile can outlive the house, but desert underlayment may last 15-20 years. Tile, foam and shingle guide for 85250.

How long does a roof last in Arizona? It depends on which layer you mean. Clay or concrete tile can last for decades, potentially longer than the house. The underlayment beneath it, which actually keeps water out, wears out much sooner in desert heat: a roofing consultant writing for Association Reserves puts it at about 15 to 20 years in desert climates, versus roughly 25 years in general.

What about foam and shingles? A Phoenix foam roofer reports spray foam roofs lasting 25-plus years when the protective coating is renewed about every five years, and asphalt shingles often showing significant age around 15 to 20 years. In 85250, where many homes date to the 1960s and '70s, the age and type of the roof is one of the first things a buyer's inspector will look at.

Data note: Lifespan ranges come from published experience, not a single study: Carl A. Brown, RCI, RRO, "Lifetime Tile Roofs: Fact or Fiction" (Association Reserves, 2012); Durafoam Roofing (Phoenix) consumer guidance; and Spray Foam Magazine (Summer 2026). Permit rules are from the City of Scottsdale. Actual life varies with installation, slope, drainage and maintenance.

Arizona Roof Lifespans at a Glance

Tile Roofs (Clay or Concrete)

  • The tile: concrete or clay "can last 100 years or more" (Association Reserves article)
  • The underlayment: about 15–20 years in desert heat (same article); a Phoenix roofer often sees it wear out around 20–30 years
  • The usual fix: a tile "lift and reset," with new underlayment and flashing and the existing tile reinstalled
  • Plan for breakage: the Association Reserves article notes about 30% of old tiles may break in a reset, so matching style and color matters

Foam Roofs (Spray Polyurethane Foam)

  • Typical life: 25+ years in Phoenix with recoats (Durafoam)
  • Recoat interval: roughly every 5 years in Phoenix sun (Durafoam)
  • Inspection: at least once a year and after major storms (Spray Foam Magazine, citing industry and manufacturer requirements)
  • Why it matters: once the coating erodes, UV degrades the exposed foam

Asphalt Shingle Roofs

  • Phoenix experience: significant age often around 15–20 years (Durafoam)
  • City of Scottsdale note: in windy areas, shingles may need replacement sooner than a 15- or 20-year warranty

Why Desert Roofs Age Differently

The roof is a system, not a surface. The City of Scottsdale's re-roofing guide breaks it into structure, sheathing, flashing, underlayment and roofing material, and calls the underlayment the roof's moisture barrier, comparing it to a carpet pad: thicker is usually better.

In the Valley, three forces do most of the damage:

  • Heat and UV, which bake underlayment and break down coatings
  • Wind, which the City calls "the most damaging" force on desert roofs
  • Water during storms, which finds every gap once the underlayment fails

The City's guide also flags attic ventilation: a closed attic expands in the heat and contracts as it cools, which can work nails loose and invite leaks.

What 85250 Buyers and Sellers Should Do

If You're Buying

  • Ask the age of each roof section and request any permits, invoices or warranties
  • Have the roof inspected during your inspection period, including flat sections
  • Look inside too: water stains at ceilings and in the attic tell their own story
  • Price the next project: if underlayment is near the end of its range, budget for a reset

If You're Selling

  • Gather records before you list: install dates, reset invoices, recoat receipts
  • Get a pre-listing roof inspection if the roof is older, so repairs are your choice, not a negotiation
  • Disclose what you know on the SPDS, including past leaks and repairs

Permits in Scottsdale

  • No permit: re-roofing with the same roofing material (City home-improvement list)
  • Permit required: if any sheathing or structural framing is repaired or replaced (City re-roofing guide)
  • Licensed contractors: the guide notes state law requires a licensed roofing contractor to warranty their installations

FAQ: Roofs in Arizona

How long does a tile roof last in Arizona?

The tile can last decades; the underlayment is the clock, about 15–20 years in desert heat by one consultant's estimate.

How long does a foam roof last in Arizona?

25+ years with a recoat about every 5 years, per a Phoenix foam roofer.

How long does a shingle roof last in Phoenix?

Often 15–20 years before showing significant age, per a Phoenix contractor.

Do I need a permit to replace my roof in Scottsdale?

Not for same-material re-roofing; yes if sheathing or framing is repaired or replaced.
