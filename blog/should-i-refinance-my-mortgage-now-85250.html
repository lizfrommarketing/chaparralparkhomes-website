Should I Refinance My Mortgage Now? A Break-Even Guide for 85250 Homeowners With Rates at 7.11%

With the 30-year rate at 7.11% (Freddie Mac, Nov. 12, 2026), who should refinance? Break-even math, CFPB red flags and what 85250 homeowners should check first.

Should you refinance your mortgage now? Only if your current rate is meaningfully higher than today's and you'll stay in the home past your break-even point. Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed average was 7.11% on Nov. 12, 2026, the fourth straight weekly decline, so most homeowners who bought in the last few years at lower rates should hold.

Who might benefit: owners who locked near the October 2023 peak of 7.79%, owners with adjustable-rate loans facing a reset, or anyone who wants to shorten the term. Everyone else should watch the weekly numbers and run the math when rates move.

Data note: Rates are from Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey (weekly release of Nov. 12, 2026, and its historical weekly data file). Refinance guidance is from the CFPB's "Should I refinance?" handout. Market figures are from Phoenix REALTORS, Scottsdale, October 2026. Read November 17, 2026.

Where Rates Stand Today

From Freddie Mac's PMMS:

  • 30-year fixed: 7.11% (Nov. 12, 2026), down from 7.18% a week earlier
  • 15-year fixed: 6.45%, down from 6.57%
  • A year ago: 6.24% for the 30-year
  • 2026 range so far: a low of 5.98% (Feb. 26) and a high of 7.40% (Oct. 8 and 15)
  • Recent peak: 7.79% on Oct. 26, 2023

The takeaway: today's rate is higher than it was for most of 2026, so the refinance window is narrow.

The Break-Even Test

The CFPB says to weigh the cost of a new loan against what you're trying to achieve. Your Loan Estimate shows the total cost, including any prepayment penalty. Then:

  1. Find your monthly savings in principal and interest
  2. Divide total costs by monthly savings to get your break-even month
  3. Compare that to how long you'll stay

A hypothetical example

These numbers are illustrative, not a quote:

  • Balance: $500,000
  • Current rate: 7.79%, about $3,596 a month in principal and interest on a 30-year schedule
  • New rate: 7.11% on a new 30-year loan, about $3,364 a month
  • Savings: about $232 a month
  • If costs total $6,000: break-even in about 26 months

One caution the CFPB highlights: a new loan restarts the clock. Lower payments for more years can mean a higher total cost.

Four Red Flags From the CFPB

The CFPB's checklist says refinancing might not make sense if:

  • You plan to move soon and won't recoup the costs
  • Your home's value has fallen since you got your loan
  • Your credit has declined, raising the rate you're offered
  • Your mortgage has a prepayment penalty

On value, 85250 owners are generally in good shape: Phoenix REALTORS reports a Scottsdale single-family median sales price of $1,225,000 in October 2026, up 5.6% year over year. Your own home's value depends on its condition and nearby sales.

Other Reasons to Refinance

Shorten the term

The CFPB notes a shorter term can mean less total interest but a higher payment. At today's averages, the 15-year rate sits 0.66 percentage points below the 30-year. The CFPB also suggests asking about a custom term, like 22 years.

Get out of an adjustable rate

If your ARM is about to adjust, locking a fixed rate can buy predictability even without big savings.

Remember what doesn't change

The CFPB reminds borrowers that property taxes and homeowner's insurance stay the same after a refinance. If taxes are your pain point, see how to lower property taxes in AZ.

If You're Planning to Sell Instead

If a move is a year or two away, a refinance rarely pays off. Put the money toward your next home instead, and see should I sell my house in the winter and how seller concessions and rate buydowns can help your buyer with today's rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I refinance my mortgage now or wait?

It depends on your rate. At 7.11%, owners near the 7.79% peak may benefit; owners at 6% to 6.5% usually should wait.

Is it worth it to refinance for 1 percent lower?

Often, if you'll stay past break-even. Divide total costs by monthly savings.

Should I refinance or just pay extra on my mortgage?

Extra principal shortens your loan without new closing costs, if there's no prepayment penalty.

When does refinancing not make sense?

When you're moving soon, your value or credit has dropped, or you'd owe a prepayment penalty.
