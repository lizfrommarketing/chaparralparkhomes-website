Arizona Winter Watering Schedule: How Often to Water Trees, Shrubs and Lawns in Scottsdale

How often should you water in an Arizona winter? Desert trees every 30–60 days, shrubs every 30–45 days. Here's the full Dec–March schedule for 85250 yards.

How often should you water in an Arizona winter? Far less than you think. From December through March, the City of Phoenix Landscape Watering Guidelines suggest established desert-adapted trees need water every 30 to 60 days, desert shrubs every 30 to 45 days, and desert groundcovers every 21 to 45 days. Cacti get water only if needed.

What's the biggest winter watering mistake? Leaving the controller on its summer schedule. The same guide notes plants can use 3 to 5 times as much water in summer as in winter, and that adjusting irrigation each season can cut landscape watering 30 to 50 percent. Change how often you water, not how long.

Data note: Intervals and depths come from the City of Phoenix Landscape Watering Guidelines and apply to established plants (about one year for shrubs, three years for trees). The guide was developed by Valley cities, including the City of Scottsdale, with University of Arizona Cooperative Extension. Freeze-timing guidance comes from UA Extension. No market statistics are included in this post.

Winter Watering Schedule at a Glance (December–March)

  • Desert-adapted trees: every 30–60 days, to 24–36 inches deep
  • High-water-use trees: every 14–30 days, to 24–36 inches
  • Desert-adapted shrubs: every 30–45 days, to 18–24 inches
  • High-water-use shrubs: every 10–14 days, to 18–24 inches
  • Desert groundcovers and vines: every 21–45 days, to 8–12 inches
  • High-water groundcovers and vines: every 10–14 days, to 8–12 inches
  • Cacti and succulents: if needed
  • Annual flowers: every 5–10 days

Rain counts. The guide says if you get at least 1/2 inch of rain, you can skip your next irrigation cycle.

How Often to Water in Fall (October–December)

November sits in the transition. The Phoenix guidelines list a fall schedule that is roughly twice as frequent as winter:

  • Desert trees: every 14–30 days; high-water trees every 7–12 days
  • Desert shrubs: every 14–30 days; high-water shrubs every 7–10 days
  • Groundcovers and vines: every 14–30 days (desert) or 7–10 days (high-water)
  • Cacti and succulents: every 21–45 days
  • Annuals: every 3–7 days

Practical approach: step down from the fall column now, then move to the winter column once nights stay cool in December.

How to Water Deeply, Not Often

Run Drip Zones Long Enough

The Phoenix guidelines note that drip run times are typically two hours or more to reach those depths. A short 15-minute cycle wets the surface but never reaches tree roots.

Water the Whole Root Zone

  • Place emitters out toward the outer edge of the canopy, not just at the trunk
  • Add emitters as trees grow; a ring that fit a young tree won't reach a mature one
  • Check depth with a soil probe or very long screwdriver about an hour after watering; it slides easily through wet soil and resists dry soil

Water in the Morning

UA Extension recommends irrigating early in the day so water soaks in before possible night freezes. That matters on frost-watch nights.

What About Lawns and Citrus?

Grass

  • Overseeded ryegrass: about every 7–14 days in winter; the monthly table lists every 10 days in November and every 14 days in December and January
  • Dormant Bermuda: every 15–30 days in winter; the guide says dormant Bermuda needs water once every 3–4 weeks or less if it rains
  • Depth: 6–10 inches, or about 3/4 inch of water per irrigation
  • New rye seed: light, frequent waterings, up to four times a day, for the first 7–10 days

See when to overseed a lawn in Scottsdale for timing.

Citrus and Fruit Trees

The Phoenix guide lists citrus as a high-water-use plant, so mature citrus fall in the 14–30 day winter range, watered to 24–36 inches.

Winter Watering and Freeze Nights

  • Water early in the day so it soaks in before possible night freezing (UA Extension)
  • Leave cacti and succulents on "if needed"
  • Protect tender plants with frost cloth. See how to protect plants from frost in Scottsdale
  • Exposed drip lines and backflow devices can be hurt by a hard freeze. See do pipes freeze in Arizona?

A Simple Controller Checklist for 85250

  • Reduce frequency, not run time
  • Set separate programs for trees, shrubs and any lawn
  • Turn on rain delay after storms
  • Walk the yard once a month for leaks, broken emitters and soggy spots
  • Reset again in March as days warm

Chaparral Park lots often mix mature shade trees with low-water desert plantings, so one-program-fits-all controllers tend to overwater one zone and starve another.

FAQ: Winter Watering in Arizona

How often should I water plants in an Arizona winter?

Desert trees: every 30–60 days. Desert shrubs: every 30–45 days.

How deep should I water trees in winter?

24–36 inches, out to the edge of the canopy.

Should I water cactus in the winter in Arizona?

Usually not. Water only if needed.

How often should I water grass in an Arizona winter?

Ryegrass: every 7–14 days. Dormant Bermuda: every 15–30 days.
