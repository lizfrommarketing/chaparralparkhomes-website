Should I Sell My House As-Is or Fix It Up? What 'As-Is' Really Means in an Arizona Sale

Should you sell your 85250 house as-is? The Arizona resale contract already sells homes in present condition, but disclosure, inspections and BINSR still apply.

Should I sell my house as-is? In Arizona, the standard Arizona REALTORS resale contract already sells every home "in its present physical condition as of the date of contract acceptance." Sellers are not obligated to negotiate repairs. So the real question is which repairs, if any, will earn back more than they cost before a buyer's inspector walks in.

What "as-is" doesn't change: you still provide a Seller's Property Disclosure Statement (SPDS), you still must disclose known latent defects, the buyer still gets an inspection period (10 days by default), and the buyer can still cancel if inspection results don't work for them. Selling as-is is a negotiating posture. It isn't a disclosure exemption.

Data note: Contract terms below come from the Arizona REALTORS Residential Resale Real Estate Purchase Contract (sample, November 2025). Terms are negotiable and can differ by transaction. No market statistics are included in this post.

What the Arizona Contract Says About Condition

Section 5a: Condition of Premises

  • The premises are sold in present physical condition as of contract acceptance
  • The seller makes no warranty about condition, except as set out elsewhere in the contract
  • Buyer and seller may, but are not obligated to, negotiate repairs
  • The seller must keep the home in substantially the same condition through possession or close of escrow, and remove debris and any personal property not included in the sale

Disclosure Still Applies

  • SPDS: delivered within 3 days after acceptance under the standard contract
  • Insurance claims history: the seller provides five years of claims history within 5 days
  • Latent defects: the seller's obligation to disclose known latent defects survives closing

How Repairs Actually Get Negotiated: The BINSR

Even on an as-is listing, buyers usually inspect. Here is the standard sequence:

  • Inspection period: 10 days after acceptance by default (negotiable)
  • Buyer's notice: the buyer can cancel or deliver a BINSR (Buyer's Inspection Notice and Seller's Response) listing disapproved items
  • Seller's response: due within 5 days. The seller can agree, refuse or offer an alternative
  • Silence counts: a seller who doesn't respond is deemed to have refused
  • Buyer's decision: within 5 days of the seller's response, the buyer can cancel or proceed
  • If repairs are agreed: they must be done in a workmanlike manner, with paid receipts delivered 3 days before close of escrow

That's why "as-is" in your listing remarks mostly sets expectations. It tells buyers you plan to answer the BINSR with a refusal, or a price conversation, rather than a repair list.

When Selling As-Is Makes Sense

  • The fix is big, and buyers would redo it anyway: a dated kitchen or full bath remodel is rarely worth starting right before a sale
  • You're out of time: an estate, relocation or second property where coordinating trades isn't practical
  • You're pricing for it: an as-is home priced with its condition already reflected tends to attract buyers who planned to renovate
  • You want fewer contingency surprises: a pre-listing inspection tells you what an inspector will find, so you can disclose it up front

When Fixing It First Usually Wins

The Items Inspectors and Lenders Flag

  • Active leaks, roof or plumbing
  • Safety items: missing GFCI protection, trip hazards, broken pool barriers
  • Systems at end of life: a failing AC or water heater becomes a negotiation either way
  • Anything an appraiser or lender may require

The Inexpensive Fixes Buyers Notice

  • Paint touch-ups, caulk and grout
  • Burned-out bulbs, sticky doors, dripping faucets
  • Yard cleanup and irrigation repairs

What Not to Fix Before Selling

  • Full remodels chosen to your taste
  • Cosmetic upgrades in rooms buyers already like
  • Anything you can't finish cleanly before photos

Small repairs remove the easy objections. Big ones usually belong in the price.

A Simple As-Is Decision Framework

  1. Get a pre-listing inspection or walk-through so you know what a buyer will see
  2. Sort items into safety, systems and cosmetic
  3. Fix safety items and quick cosmetics; get bids for the big-ticket items
  4. Price with the remaining items in mind, and disclose them
  5. Decide your BINSR posture before the first offer arrives

FAQ: Selling As-Is in Arizona

Can I sell my house as-is in Arizona?

Yes. The standard contract sells homes in present condition.

Do you have to fix everything on a home inspection in Arizona?

No. The seller can agree, refuse or counter on the BINSR.

What is a BINSR in real estate?

The Buyer's Inspection Notice and Seller's Response.

Does selling as-is mean I don't have to disclose problems?

No. The SPDS and latent defect disclosure still apply.
