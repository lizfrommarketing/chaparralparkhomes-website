How Often to Replace Smoke Detectors: The 10-Year Rule, Monthly Tests and a Holiday Checklist for 85250 Homes

Replace smoke alarms 10 years from the date printed on the back, test monthly and change 9-volt or backup batteries yearly. NFPA and U.S. Fire Administration guidance for 85250 homes.

How often should you replace smoke detectors? Replace every smoke alarm 10 years after the manufacture date printed on the back of the unit, even if it still beeps when you test it. Test each alarm once a month, and change 9-volt batteries and the backup batteries in hardwired alarms at least once a year.

Why it matters this month: The holidays bring more cooking, candles and guests. NFPA research shows that roughly three out of five home fire deaths happen in homes with no smoke alarms or no working alarms, and that working alarms cut the risk of dying in a home fire in half.

Data note: Guidance and statistics are from the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) smoke alarm page and the U.S. Fire Administration (USFA) smoke alarm page, both read November 23, 2026.

The Three Numbers to Remember

Monthly: test

  • Press the test button on every alarm, once a month
  • You should hear a loud beep, beep, beep
  • No sound means a new battery, or a new alarm if the unit is over 10 years old

Yearly: batteries

  • 9-volt alarms: replace the battery at least once a year
  • Hardwired alarms: replace the backup battery at least once a year
  • Sealed 10-year lithium alarms: no battery changes; replace the whole unit per the manufacturer

Ten years: the whole alarm

  • NFPA: all smoke alarms must be replaced 10 years after the manufacturing date
  • USFA says the same: smoke alarms "need to be replaced 10 years from the manufacture date"
  • Look for the date on the back. If there is no date, the alarm is almost certainly past due

How to Read What Your Alarm Is Telling You

  • Three loud beeps, repeating: smoke or fire. Get out, call 9-1-1 and stay out
  • A single chirp every 30 or 60 seconds: low battery
  • Chirping after a new battery: the alarm is at the end of its life. Replace it

Many 85250 homes were built in the 1960s and 1970s, and most have been remodeled at least once since. That often means a mix of alarm ages in one house: a newer hardwired unit in the kitchen remodel, and older ones down the hall. Check every date, not just one.

Where Smoke Alarms Belong

Both NFPA and USFA recommend the same layout:

  • Inside every bedroom and outside each sleeping area, such as the hallway
  • On every level of the home
  • On the ceiling or high on a wall
  • At least 10 feet from the stove to cut down on nuisance alarms
  • Not in kitchens or bathrooms

Interconnected alarms are best: when one sounds, they all sound. That matters in single-level ranch homes with a primary suite on one end and guest rooms on the other. Only qualified electricians should install hardwired alarms.

Which Type to Buy

There are two basic sensor types:

  • Ionization alarms respond faster to flaming fires
  • Photoelectric alarms respond faster to smoldering fires, and NFPA notes they are the better choice near kitchens and bathrooms

USFA recommends having both types or dual-sensor alarms. Its cost guide lists ionization and photoelectric alarms from about $6 and up, dual-sensor from $24 and up, and wireless interconnected alarms from $40 and up. NFPA also points to newer multi-criteria alarms, labeled as helping reduce cooking nuisance alarms, when it's time to replace.

A 15-Minute Holiday Checklist

  1. Find the date on the back of every alarm and write it down
  2. Test each one with the button
  3. Swap 9-volt and backup batteries if you can't remember the last change
  4. Order replacements for any alarm over 10 years old, or with no date
  5. Never pull a battery to stop a cooking alarm. USFA says to open a window, press hush and fan the air instead
  6. Walk your escape plan with houseguests. NFPA notes you may have as little as 2 minutes to get out

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should smoke detectors be replaced?

Every 10 years from the manufacture date on the back, per NFPA and USFA.

How often should you replace smoke detector batteries?

At least once a year for 9-volt and hardwired backup batteries. Sealed 10-year alarms are replaced as a whole unit.

How do I test a smoke detector?

Press the test button and listen for the loud three-beep pattern. Test monthly.

Why is my smoke detector chirping after I changed the battery?

That usually means the alarm has reached the end of its life. Replace the unit.
