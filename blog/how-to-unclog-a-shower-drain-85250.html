How to Unclog a Shower Drain: Hair, Standing Water and Hard-Water Buildup, a Step-by-Step Guide for Scottsdale 85250 Homes

Most shower clogs are hair and soap. Pull it out, use a plastic drain tool or plunger, then a hand snake. Why Poison Control urges care with drain cleaners, and when it's a sewer issue.

How do you unclog a shower drain? Take off the drain cover, pull out the hair you can reach, then plunge. If water still won't drain, run a hand drain snake down the pipe and flush with hot tap water. Most shower clogs are hair bound together with soap residue, and they usually sit close to the drain opening.

Skip the fizzing trick: Poison Control says baking soda and vinegar usually can't build enough pressure to clear a real clog, because the gas escapes through the drain cover. And harsh drain cleaners carry real burn risk if they splash.

Data note: Drain cleaner safety and the baking soda explanation are from Poison Control (National Capital Poison Center). Sewer responsibility is from Scottsdale Water's maintenance and troubleshooting page. Hardness is from the City of Scottsdale 2026 Water Quality Report. Read November 24, 2026. What You'll Need

  • Rubber gloves and a flashlight
  • Screwdriver for the drain cover
  • Plastic barbed drain tool, the inexpensive zip-strip kind
  • Cup plunger
  • Hand drain snake (auger); a short household one is usually enough for a shower
  • Hair catcher for afterward

Step-by-Step

1. Remove the cover

Most shower strainers unscrew or pop off. Tub drains may have a stopper you lift and twist out.

2. Pull the hair

Reach in with gloves or feed the plastic barbed tool down, then pull up slowly. This clears a large share of slow drains on its own.

3. Plunge

Leave enough water over the drain to cover the plunger's rim so it seals. For a tub, cover the overflow opening with a wet rag first, then give it a series of firm pushes.

4. Snake it

Feed the auger in, crank when you feel resistance, and pull back out. Repeat until water flows freely.

5. Flush and protect

Run hot tap water, reinstall the cover and add a hair catcher.

Got Standing Water?

  • Bail it out with a cup before using a drain tool so you can see what you're doing
  • Don't pour chemical cleaner into standing water; Poison Control notes splash-back risk rises when too much product goes into a completely blocked drain
  • Still standing after snaking? It may be deeper in the line. Time to call a plumber

About Chemical Drain Cleaners

Poison Control explains that non-enzymatic drain cleaners use acids (like sulfuric or hydrochloric) or alkalis (like sodium or potassium hydroxide). If you use one:

  • Wear goggles and rubber gloves
  • Pour slowly and close to the drain
  • Keep containers closed and out of children's reach
  • For any exposure, Poison Control is free and confidential at 1-800-222-1222, 24 hours a day

Enzymatic cleaners are milder but slower and work best as maintenance.

The Hard-Water Factor in 85250

Scottsdale Water's 2026 report lists approximate hardness of 13 to 17 grains per gallon (about 215 to 285 mg/L) between Indian School and Chaparral roads, and 13 to 17 gpg north of Chaparral Road. Minerals plus soap leave scum that catches hair. Regular hot-water flushes and a clean strainer help. More in our hard water guide.

When It's a Sewer Problem

Watch for more than one drain backing up, gurgling or water rising in the shower when you flush. Scottsdale Water says:

  • The city maintains the sewer main
  • The owner is responsible for the sewer lateral, the pipe from the main to the house
  • The city can send a crew to help find where a backup starts: 480-312-5650

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I unclog a shower drain?

Remove the cover, pull the hair, plunge, then snake if needed.

Does baking soda and vinegar unclog a shower drain?

Usually not for a true clog, per Poison Control.

Are chemical drain cleaners safe to use?

Use with goggles and gloves, pour close to the drain, and keep away from kids.

When is a slow shower drain a bigger problem?

When several drains back up at once. Call a plumber or Scottsdale Water.
