How to Get Rid of Roof Rats in Arizona: Signs, Prevention and Help for Scottsdale and 85250 Homes

Roof rats are most active in the Valley from November through May. How to spot them, seal them out, manage citrus and trash, and who to call in Scottsdale.

How do you get rid of roof rats in Arizona? Seal every gap into the house, remove easy food and harborage, and use traps or a licensed pest professional for the rats already inside. University of Arizona Cooperative Extension says baits and traps alone give only short-term control; exclusion and habitat changes are the long-term fix.

Why now: UA Extension notes roof rats in Valley cities are far more active from November through May, the months when backyard citrus is loaded with fruit. In mature, irrigated neighborhoods like Chaparral Park, that makes late fall the right time to inspect your roofline.

Data note: Guidance is from University of Arizona Cooperative Extension publications AZ1775, AZ1677 and AZ1784, UA's "How to Identify and Discourage Roof Rats" (October 2024), and Maricopa County Environmental Services. Read November 17, 2026.

What Roof Rats Are and Why They're in the Valley

  • Species: Rattus rattus, also called the black rat or ship rat, is not native to Arizona
  • Arrival: UA Extension says roof rats were documented in Phoenix in 2001
  • Habitat: they favor old citrus, palm trees and mature landscaping near irrigation and canals
  • Size: adults are about 5 to 7 inches, roughly 12 inches including the tail, which is usually longer than the body
  • Access: they can squeeze through openings as small as a dime and run along utility lines

Signs You Have Roof Rats

UA Extension lists these clues:

  • Droppings that are elongated with large tapered ends
  • Gnaw marks on wood, wiring, insulation or stored items
  • Oily rub marks along beams and walls
  • Noises in the attic or walls, usually at night
  • Hollowed-out citrus on the tree or the ground, with the rind left behind
  • Twilight sightings on fences, roofs or overhead lines

Step 1: Seal Them Out

Exclusion is the long-term solution, according to UA's pest-proofing guide (AZ1677):

  • Close gaps from ground to roof. A gap of half an inch is enough for a young rat.
  • Focus on utility penetrations: air-conditioning lines, water pipes, electrical and cable entries
  • Use durable materials: cement, mortar or outdoor-rated sealants. Steel or copper wool alone is only temporary.
  • Cover vents on the attic, roof and chimney with ¼-inch hardware cloth, wearing gloves because the cut edges are sharp
  • Check door sweeps and thresholds, including the garage

Step 2: Take Away Food and Shelter

  • Pick up fallen fruit and nuts promptly and harvest citrus completely; see when to pick oranges in Arizona
  • Trim branches so they don't touch walls or the roofline
  • Keep trash lids closed and steam clean bins at least twice a year, per UA
  • Bring pet food in at night
  • Clear stored materials and wood piles where rats nest

One myth worth dropping: UA Extension says research shows roof rat populations aren't tied to food plants, so removing fruit trees won't solve the problem. Good pruning and cleanup will help.

Step 3: Deal With Rats Already Inside

  • Traps: place them where you see droppings or gnaw marks, keeping them away from children and pets
  • Baits: if used, choose products labeled for rats and follow the label exactly to protect pets and wildlife
  • Hire a professional for attics, wall voids or repeat activity. Look for a licensed pest control company.
  • Clean up safely: UA's AZ1784 lists diseases roof rats are implicated in, including leptospirosis, salmonellosis, rat-bite fever and murine typhus. Wear gloves and avoid stirring up dust.

Why speed matters: UA notes females have 5 to 8 pups per litter, up to 5 litters a year, and young rats can breed at about three months.

Who to Call in Scottsdale

Maricopa County Environmental Services' Vector Control Division investigates roof rat complaints to identify the responsible party. Call 602-506-6616. The county does not provide bait stations or traps to the public.

Roof rats are a different problem from native pack rats; for those, see how to get rid of pack rats in Arizona.

What It Means When You Buy or Sell

  • Sellers: disclose known rodent activity and repairs on the SPDS; see Arizona's seller disclosure
  • Buyers: ask your inspector to note droppings, damaged insulation and gaps at the roofline
  • Everyone: sealed vents and trimmed trees are inexpensive proof of good upkeep

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I have roof rats?

Look for tapered droppings, gnaw marks, attic noises and citrus hollowed out with the rind left behind.

What attracts roof rats to your house?

Fallen fruit, open trash, pet food and branches touching the roof. Trim, clean up and seal gaps.

Are roof rats dangerous?

They can chew wiring and are implicated in several diseases, per UA Extension. Clean up with gloves.

Who do I call about roof rats in Scottsdale?

Maricopa County Vector Control investigates complaints at 602-506-6616; hire a licensed pest company for removal.
