Can I Sell My House With a Lien on It? Arizona Rules for Tax, Judgment and Contractor Liens

Yes, you can usually sell a house with a lien in Arizona. Liens are typically paid from proceeds at closing. How mortgage, IRS, judgment and contractor liens work.

Can I sell my house with a lien on it? Usually, yes. In Arizona, most liens are paid off from your sale proceeds through escrow at closing, so the buyer gets clear title and you receive what's left. Your mortgage is a lien too, and sellers pay those off at closing every day.

The real question is math and timing. Does the price cover every lien plus your costs of sale? And has each lienholder given a payoff, release or discharge in time for closing? IRS liens, judgments and contractor liens each follow their own rules, outlined below.

Data note: Rules below summarize Arizona Revised Statutes §§ 33-964, 33-993 and 33-1101 and IRS Publication 783, read November 9, 2026. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

Selling With a Lien: Key Facts

  • Title search first: your title company finds recorded liens when you open escrow
  • Proceeds pay liens: escrow pays lienholders from the sale, in priority order, before you're paid
  • Judgment liens: last 10 years from the judgment date in the county where recorded (A.R.S. § 33-964)
  • Homestead protection: Arizona's homestead exemption is set at $400,000 in statute and adjusted annually for inflation since Jan. 1, 2024 (A.R.S. § 33-1101)
  • Contractor liens: must be recorded within 120 days after completion, or 60 days after a recorded notice of completion (A.R.S. § 33-993)
  • IRS liens: apply for a Certificate of Discharge with Form 14135, ideally 45+ days before closing (IRS Pub. 783)

Step 1: Find Out What's Actually Recorded

Start with a preliminary title report. When you open escrow, the title company searches county records and lists every recorded lien on your property. Common ones include:

  • Mortgage and HELOC deeds of trust
  • Judgment liens from lawsuits or unpaid debts
  • Mechanics' liens from contractors or suppliers
  • Federal tax liens from the IRS
  • HOA assessment liens, where an association applies

A lien you didn't know about can surface late. Better to find it before you list than in week three of escrow. If a recorded document looks unfamiliar or fraudulent, our guide to Arizona deed fraud protection explains the free Maricopa Title Alert.

Step 2: Understand How Each Lien Type Gets Cleared

Mortgage and HELOC liens

Escrow orders payoff statements from your lenders and pays the balances from your proceeds. The lender then releases its deed of trust. This is routine.

Judgment liens and the homestead exemption

Under A.R.S. § 33-964, a recorded judgment becomes a lien on your real property in that county for ten years. State civil judgment liens and support liens remain until satisfied.

When you sell your homestead, the statute sets the payment order: the judgment creditor is paid after the homestead exemption amount goes to you and after any higher-priority liens. A.R.S. § 33-1101 sets the exemption at $400,000 of equity, adjusted annually for inflation. The exemption also follows identifiable cash proceeds for 18 months after the sale or until you buy a new homestead, whichever comes first. If your expected net is below the exemption, § 33-964 lets a title insurer use a partial release or extinguishment process. That process involves notices sent at least 20 days before the sale is final.

Contractor (mechanics') liens

Under A.R.S. § 33-993, a contractor or supplier must record a sworn notice and claim of lien within 120 days after completion. If a notice of completion was recorded, the deadline is 60 days. The claimant then serves a copy on the owner. Before listing, gather lien waivers from any recent remodel. A disputed lien can still delay closing, even when the dispute is valid.

Federal tax liens

IRS Publication 783 explains how to get the government's lien removed from a specific property through a Certificate of Discharge under Internal Revenue Code § 6325(b). You apply on Form 14135, and the IRS asks you to submit it at least 45 days before the sale. Attachments include the sale contract, a title report, a valuation and a proposed closing statement. Depending on the section you apply under, the IRS may require payment from proceeds as a condition.

Step 3: Run the Net-Proceeds Math

Add up every lien payoff plus your costs of sale, including commissions, title, escrow and any prorations, and compare the total to a realistic price. Then you know where you stand:

  • Price covers everything: escrow pays all liens and you net the rest.
  • Price is close: negotiate payoffs. Some creditors accept less to get paid now.
  • Price falls short: bring cash to closing, request a discharge, or talk with the lender about a short sale. Get legal or tax advice first.

Step 4: Disclose and Plan the Timeline

Tell your agent and escrow officer about every lien you know of on day one. Arizona resale contracts also call for a Seller's Property Disclosure Statement. Our Arizona SPDS guide covers what it asks. Build extra days into the contract if an IRS discharge or a judgment negotiation is in play.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sell my house with a lien on it in Arizona?

Usually, yes. Escrow pays liens from your proceeds at closing. If proceeds fall short, you'll need a negotiated payoff, cash or a release.

Can I sell my house with an IRS lien?

Yes. Apply for a Certificate of Discharge on Form 14135, ideally at least 45 days before closing (IRS Pub. 783).

How long does a judgment lien last in Arizona?

Ten years from the judgment date in the county where it's recorded (A.R.S. § 33-964). State civil and support liens last until satisfied.

How long does a contractor have to file a lien in Arizona?

120 days after completion, or 60 days after a recorded notice of completion (A.R.S. § 33-993).
