How Do I Know What to Sell My House For? Pricing an 85250 Home in Fall 2026

How do you know what to sell your house for in 85250? Start with sold $/SF ($454.34), not list prices. Homes sold at 97.09% of list (Cromford).

How do I know what to sell my house for? Start with what comparable homes have sold for per square foot, not what neighbors are asking. In ZIP 85250, the Cromford Report shows a median annual sold price of $454.34 per square foot for single-family homes, with homes under contract at $483.51 and active listings at $476.42 (measured 10/18/2026).

How much should I list my house for? Close to where buyers are actually signing. Homes in 85250 sold at an average of 97.09% of list price, down from 100.36% a month earlier. Buyers are negotiating harder than they were in September, so a home priced above the evidence tends to sit, then chase the market down.

Data note: 85250 figures are from the Cromford Report, ZIP 85250, single-family detached homes only, measured 10/18/2026 (condos and townhomes not included). LM = last month (9/18/2026); LY = last year (10/18/2025). Data change daily.

How to Use These Numbers to Price Your Home

Step 1: Anchor to Sold, Then Check Under Contract

Sold prices are facts. They're also history: the annual median blends 12 months of closings. Under-contract $/SF is the freshest signal you can see, though it can change before closing. In 85250 right now, the two tell a consistent story: buyers are signing at $483.51 per square foot, in line with active asking prices ($476.42) and above the 12-month sold median.

Step 2: Choose the Right Comparables

A ZIP-wide median is a starting point, not your price. Comparables should match:

  • Street and setting: proximity to Chaparral Park, the greenbelt, busy roads
  • Size and layout: bedrooms, bathrooms, single-level vs. two-story
  • Condition and updates: original 1970s kitchen vs. a full remodel
  • Lot and outdoor space: pool, views, yard size
  • Timing: the most recent sales first, since the market shifted between September and October

Step 3: Do the Math, Then Sanity-Check It

Illustration only, not a valuation: at the annual median of $454.34, an 1,800-square-foot home works out to about $817,800. At the under-contract median of $483.51, it's about $870,300. That $52,500 spread is why comps matter more than any single average.

Step 4: Price for the Negotiation You'll Actually Get

With homes closing about 2.9% below list on average (100% − 97.09%), a list price well above your comps doesn't create room to negotiate. It mostly reduces showings.

Should I Reduce My House Price? Watch These Signals

  • Showings without offers as you approach the market's typical pace (a 58-day median to first contract in 85250)
  • Repeated feedback about price rather than condition
  • New, lower-priced competition for the same buyer
  • Buyers going under contract nearby while yours sits

If a well-presented home gets showings but no offers, the price is usually the message. One decisive adjustment tends to work better than several small ones.
