Arizona Winter Forecast 2026: What a Very Strong El Niño Could Mean for Scottsdale and 85250 Homes

NOAA's 2026-27 winter outlook favors a wetter Southwest as a very strong El Niño builds. What it could mean for Scottsdale and how to prep an 85250 home.

What is the Arizona winter forecast for 2026? NOAA's 2026-27 Winter Outlook, released Oct. 15, 2026, favors wetter-than-average conditions across the Southwest for December through February. The reason is a very strong El Niño: NOAA's Climate Prediction Center gives a greater than 83% chance of a strong-to-very-strong event through January-March 2027. Southwest temperatures are rated equal chances.

What it means for 85250: better odds of real winter rain, plus a few weeks in which storms arrive back to back. It isn't a guarantee of a wet winter. It is a good reason to check your roof, drainage and flood coverage before January.

Data note: Forecast details come from NOAA's Oct. 15, 2026 Winter Outlook release, the Climate Prediction Center's Oct. 8 ENSO discussion and Oct. 15 seasonal outlook discussion, the University of Arizona CLIMAS El Niño 2026-27 factsheet, KJZZ reporting (Sept. 9, 2026) and the City of Scottsdale's emergency preparedness page, read November 10, 2026. The next ENSO update is scheduled for Nov. 12.

The 2026-27 Outlook at a Glance

  • Precipitation (Dec.-Feb.): wetter than average is favored across the southern tier, including the Southwest
  • Temperature: much of the Southwest has equal chances of below-, near- or above-average
  • Drought: NOAA's drought outlook through January favors improvement or removal in the Southwest
  • El Niño strength: Niño-3.4 reached +2.1°C in the Oct. 8 CPC discussion; weekly values hit +2.4°C by the Oct. 15 outlook
  • Historic potential: CPC put the odds of exceeding every El Niño since 1950 at 83% for October-December
  • Peak window for Arizona: January-February, per UA's CLIMAS team

Why El Niño Matters for Arizona Winters

During El Niño, the Pacific jet stream tends to shift south, steering more storms across the southern U.S. NOAA's National Weather Service director Ken Graham described it as a shift that "tilts the odds toward stormier weather across the South."

It's odds, not a promise

The CLIMAS factsheet is direct: El Niño does not guarantee a wet winter. Two very strong events show the range:

  • 1997-98: increased rainfall in the region
  • 2015-16: drier conditions in Arizona, as storms tracked farther north

KJZZ reported that CPC's long-range outlook gives most of Arizona a 33% to 60% chance of above-normal rainfall this winter.

Storms in bursts

UA's Michael Crimmins told KJZZ that past El Niños brought weeks of storms running into Arizona "over and over again," followed by sunny breaks. CLIMAS also flags greater potential for heavy precipitation and localized flooding.

How to Prep an 85250 Home Before January

A few hours of prep now goes a long way, especially if your house has flat or low-slope roof sections that drain through scuppers.

Roof and drainage

  • Clear scuppers, gutters and downspouts of leaves and palm debris
  • Look at the roof after the next wind event. On flat roofs, check for ponding and cracked coating. Our guide to how long a roof lasts in Arizona covers typical lifespans
  • Check grading: soil and rock should slope away from the foundation, not toward it
  • Test yard drains and make sure pool and patio areas don't push water toward the house

Flood risk and insurance

The Indian Bend Wash greenbelt is a flood-control channel as well as a park. Look up your parcel's flood zone and ask your agent about flood coverage before storms arrive. Our post on buying a house in a flood zone in Scottsdale walks through the maps.

Sandbags

The City of Scottsdale says it may make sand and sandbags available during seasonal storms at self-service locations:

  • District 1 Police Station: Miller Road & McKellips
  • Scottsdale Water Campus: 88th Street & Union Hills Drive

Cold snaps still happen

Clear nights between storms can turn cold. See our guides on whether pipes freeze in Arizona and whether Scottsdale gets snow.
