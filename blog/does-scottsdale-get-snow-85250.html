Does Scottsdale Get Snow? Climate Facts for Chaparral Park and ZIP 85250 Buyers

Does Scottsdale get snow? Almost never in the Valley. NWS Phoenix says measurable snow is rare below 2,000 feet. Winters are mild; mountains get the real snow.

Does Scottsdale get snow? Almost never at the elevations that matter for Chaparral Park and most of ZIP 85250. The National Weather Service Phoenix office says that in most of the metro area, an event that produces measurable snow is still quite rare. The official Phoenix reporting station's greatest recorded totals are 1.0 inch on January 20, 1933, and again on January 21–22, 1937.

Does it ever snow? Trace flurries show up more often than a measurable accumulation, and NWS notes that snowfall reports are becoming more common in higher foothill neighborhoods of the north Valley. For central Scottsdale, including the Chaparral Park area around Hayden and Chaparral, winter is about mild days, cool nights and rare frost, not shovels and snow days.

Data note: Snow history comes from NWS Phoenix's "History of Snowfall in Phoenix." Temperature and freeze-day normals come from the Arizona State Climate Office Scottsdale climate summary (NOAA 1991–2020 normals). No local real estate market statistics are included.

Scottsdale Winter Climate at a Glance

  • Measurable Valley snow: rare; official Phoenix station max 1.0 inch (1933 and 1937) per NWS Phoenix
  • Most recent significant event below 2,000 feet called out by NWS: December 6, 1998 (Sky Harbor recorded 0.22 inches of precipitation that day, but only a trace of snow)
  • January normals (Scottsdale): average high 66.5°F, low 43.4°F, mean 54.9°F
  • December normals: average high 64.6°F, low 42.5°F, mean 53.6°F
  • Days with min 32°F or below: about 3 per year (Scottsdale normals)
  • Annual precipitation normal: 8.73 inches
  • Annual mean temperature: 73.2°F

What "Snow in Scottsdale" Usually Means

When local news mentions snow in the Phoenix metro area, NWS Phoenix's history page is the best reality check:

  • Most of the city: measurable snow remains quite rare
  • Higher foothill elevations in the north Valley: snowfall reports are becoming more common as the metro expands into those areas
  • Trace amounts have been recorded on numerous occasions
  • Other official measurable totals at Phoenix include 0.5 inches (Feb. 2, 1939), 0.4 inches (Dec. 21–22, 1990), 0.2 inches (March 12, 1917), and 0.1 inches (Nov. 28, 1919 and Dec. 11, 1985)

For practical living in 85250, that means you plan for occasional frost, not snow removal. If you want a weekend in real snow, you drive to the mountains.

Valley Floor vs. Mountains

Elevation is the whole story. Chaparral Park sits on the Valley floor. Higher terrain north and east of Scottsdale cools faster and catches storms that never stick as snow downtown.

NWS Phoenix specifically separates:

  1. Areas below about 2,000 feet — significant snow is uncommon; the December 1998 event is the most recent one they highlight for those elevations
  2. Higher foothills — more frequent snow reports as development moves uphill
  3. Arizona's mountain communities — the places that actually build a winter around snow

So if a listing photo shows a snow-dusted McDowell or Superstition ridge while your backyard is dry, that is normal, not a contradiction.

What Winter Actually Feels Like in 85250

Arizona State Climate Office normals for Scottsdale (period of record noted on the sheet; normals 1991–2020) paint a clear picture:

  • Daytime winter highs typically sit in the mid-60s
  • Overnight lows often sit in the low-to-mid 40s
  • Hard freezes are limited: roughly three mornings a year at or below 32°F
  • Rain, not snow, is the winter precipitation story, with annual normals around 8.73 inches

That climate is why November through March is prime patio season here — and why homebuyers from snow states often ask this exact question. Related reading: how to protect plants from frost, do pipes freeze in Arizona, and Arizona winter watering schedule.

Frost, Not Snow: What Still Matters in Winter

Even without snow days, 85250 winters are not a free pass on cold-weather prep:

  • Citrus and tender plants can still burn on those few nights at or below freezing — see how to protect plants from frost in Scottsdale
  • Exposed pipes on older ranch homes deserve a glance before a hard freeze — see do pipes freeze in Arizona
  • Pool equipment benefits from freeze-protection settings even when snow is not in the forecast
  • Morning commute fog or slick spots are uncommon compared with snow states, but shade lines can hold frost briefly after a clear, calm night

Annual precipitation normals sit at 8.73 inches — rain measured in tenths of an inch most winter months, not snowpack.

Buying Notes for Relocators

If you are comparing Scottsdale to a snow-belt city, price in these climate realities — not lifestyle stereotypes:

  • No routine snow removal budget for sidewalks or driveways in most of 85250
  • Frost protection for citrus, pipes and pool equipment still matters on those few cold mornings
  • Mountain day trips are how many residents get snow recreation without living in it
  • Fair housing reminder: climate is a property and location fact. It is not a coded way to describe who a neighborhood is "for."

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Scottsdale get snow?

Almost never at Valley elevations. NWS Phoenix says measurable snow is still quite rare in most of the metro area. The official Phoenix station's greatest totals are 1.0 inch in 1933 and 1937.

Does Scottsdale ever get snow?

Rarely. Trace flurries happen more often than measurable accumulation, and higher north Valley foothills see snowfall reports more often than central Scottsdale.

How cold does Scottsdale get in winter?

January average highs are 66.5°F and lows 43.4°F per Arizona State Climate Office / NOAA normals. Scottsdale averages about three days a year with a minimum of 32°F or below.

Where can I see snow near Scottsdale?

Higher elevations north of the Valley. Mountain communities regularly see winter snow while Scottsdale and Phoenix stay mostly snow-free.
