How to Find Your Property Lines in Maricopa County (and When You Need a Survey)

How do you find property lines in Maricopa County? Start with your recorded plat and deed, use the Assessor map as a guide, and hire a surveyor for exact pins.

How do you find your property lines in Maricopa County? Start with your recorded subdivision plat. The Maricopa County Assessor says that in a platted subdivision, the recorded plat is the authoritative source for lot lines and dimensions, and you can reach it through the MCR link on your parcel's property information page. If your lot isn't platted, use the legal description in your deed.

Can I just use the parcel map? As a guide, yes. As proof, no. The Assessor says parcel lines on its viewer are illustrative only, and its labeled dimensions aren't authoritative. To find the actual pins in the ground, you need a registered land surveyor. The Assessor doesn't measure properties or locate pins.

Ways to Find Property Lines at a Glance

  • Assessor Parcel Viewer: free, quick, illustrative only
  • Recorded plat (MCR): authoritative in platted subdivisions
  • Deed legal description: the backup when there's no plat or it's been modified
  • Registered land surveyor: the only way to locate or set pins
  • Copies from the Recorder: $1 per page; $3 to certify a document

Step 1: Look Up Your Parcel

On the Assessor's Parcel Viewer, search your address or parcel number (APN).

  • The map shows an approximate outline of your lot
  • The Parcel Labels layer shows dimensions, but the Assessor says they aren't authoritative
  • Your property information page lists the subdivision, MCR (plat) number and deed number

Google Maps and phone apps that draw lot lines aren't official records either, so treat them as estimates.

Step 2: Read the Recorded Plat

If your home is in a recorded subdivision, the plat is your best free document.

What a Plat Shows

  • Lot dimensions and bearings
  • Easements, such as public utility easements along rear or side lot lines
  • Street rights-of-way and common tracts

Where the Deed Comes In

If your lot isn't platted, has been split or combined, or the plat lacks the dimensions you need, the Assessor points you to the legal description in your deed. Follow the deed link on your parcel page, or order a copy from the Recorder.

Step 3: Hire a Surveyor When It Matters

When a Survey Is Worth It

  • Building a fence, wall, pool, casita or addition near a line
  • A neighbor disagreement about where the line is
  • Missing or buried pins you want located or reset
  • Buying a home where fences don't match the plat

How to Verify a Surveyor

Arizona's Board of Technical Registration offers a Registered Professional Search. Select "Land Surveyor" to confirm a registration is current before you hire.

Fences and Walls in Scottsdale

Scottsdale's Walls and Fences page (updated May 21, 2026) says a permit is required for:

  • Any fence or wall taller than 3 feet
  • Adding height to an existing wall
  • Any retaining wall (engineered plans required)
  • Dry-stack or interlocking walls over 30 inches
  • Replacing an existing fence

Fences 3 feet or shorter don't need a permit, but the city offers a courtesy site plan review at the One Stop Shop, 7447 E. Indian School Road, to check for easements and drainage. Building in a public utility easement requires written approval from every utility.

Property Lines When You Buy or Sell

The Arizona REALTORS resale contract addresses this directly:

  • Lot size and square footage are approximate; buyers should verify during the inspection period
  • Brokers are not responsible for verifying lot lines or boundaries
  • Buyers get 5 days after receiving the title commitment to review exceptions, including recorded easements

If a wall or fence looks off, raise it during inspection. Worried about title fraud? See Arizona deed fraud protection.

“Older Chaparral Park lots sometimes have block walls that were built decades ago and never quite match the plat. Before a buyer plans a pool or casita, I tell them to pull the plat first, then budget for a survey if they're building anywhere near a line. It's cheaper than moving a wall.”
— Elizabeth Vijan, Tackett Team, eXp Realty

FAQ: Property Lines in Maricopa County

How do I find my property lines in Maricopa County for free?

Use the Assessor Parcel Viewer and your recorded plat.

Are the Assessor's parcel map lines accurate?

Not for boundaries. They're illustrative only.

Do I need a survey to build a fence in Scottsdale?

Not always, but fences over 3 feet need a permit.

How do I get a copy of my deed in Maricopa County?

From the Recorder: $1 per page, $3 to certify.
