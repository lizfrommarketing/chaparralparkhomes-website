When to Fertilize Citrus Trees in Arizona: The University of Arizona Schedule for Scottsdale and 85250 Yards

University of Arizona Extension: feed oranges and grapefruit in Jan-Feb, Mar-Apr and May-June; lemons and limes in Jan-Feb, Mar-Apr and Aug-Sept. How much, and why not in November.

When should you fertilize citrus trees in Arizona? Three times a year, in windows that depend on the fruit. The University of Arizona Cooperative Extension recommends splitting each tree's annual fertilizer into thirds: oranges, tangerines and grapefruit in January-February, March-April and May-June, and lemons and limes in January-February, March-April and August-September.

What about right now? Mid-November isn't on anyone's calendar. Every citrus feeding window is closed until January, so this is the season to harvest, water wisely and protect young trees from frost, then feed on schedule after the new year.

Data note: Timing and rates come from the University of Arizona Cooperative Extension's Citrus Fertilization Chart for Arizona and its "Citrus Care for the Homeowner" presentation by Glenn Wright. Read November 13, 2026.

The Arizona Citrus Fertilizer Calendar

Oranges, tangerines, grapefruit and other non-lemon citrus:

  • January-February: one-third of the annual amount
  • March-April: one-third
  • May-June: one-third
  • July-December: none

Lemons and limes:

  • January-February: one-third
  • March-April: one-third
  • August-September: one-third

Easy reminder: the Arizona Landscape Contractors Association notes the old rule of thumb was "Valentine's Day, Memorial Day and Labor Day" for all citrus. The Extension's updated chart keeps that rhythm but moves the last orange and grapefruit feeding earlier, to May-June, and gives you wider windows in case you miss a date.

How Much to Use

The Extension bases the amount on tree size, not age, measured in pounds of actual nitrogen per year:

  • Newly planted: small amounts only after new growth emerges, less than 0.25 lb.
  • 2 to 3 feet tall: 0.25 to 0.50 lb.
  • 4 to 8 feet tall: 1.00 to 1.50 lb.
  • 10 feet or taller: 1.75 to 2.5 lb.

Adjust by fruit:

  • Grapefruit: about 50% less (1.5 lb. maximum, per the Extension presentation)
  • Lemons and limes: about 10% more
  • Sandy or gravelly soil: a little more

Turning nitrogen into pounds of fertilizer

The first number on the bag is the percent nitrogen. A 13-10-4 citrus food is 13% nitrogen. Divide the pounds of nitrogen your tree needs by that percentage to get pounds of product. For example, 1 lb. of nitrogen from a 13% fertilizer is about 7.7 lb. of product for the year, or roughly 2.6 lb. per feeding. The Extension's chart does this math for common percentages.

How to Apply It

  • Spread it under the canopy, keeping it off the trunk
  • Work it into the soil and water it in right away; the Extension says to always follow with irrigation
  • Pull weeds and grass under the tree; the Extension presentation notes they compete for water and nutrients
  • Phosphorus is usually covered by a complete citrus food; if applied separately, the Extension suggests once a year, in March or October
  • Liquid fertilizers work too, according to the Extension

Common Citrus Fertilizing Mistakes

  • Feeding oranges or grapefruit in the fall: the Extension's chart calls for none from July through December
  • Guessing at amounts: measure accurately; the chart is built around pounds of actual nitrogen
  • Treating every tree the same: grapefruit need about half, lemons and limes about 10% more
  • Skipping the water: granular fertilizer should be worked in and irrigated right away
  • Over-feeding a new tree: wait until it's established and pushing new growth

What to Do in November Instead

  • Harvest when the fruit tastes ready: see when to pick oranges in Arizona
  • Plan frost cover for young trees and lemons: how to protect plants from frost
  • Shift to the winter watering schedule: Arizona winter watering
  • Put three reminders in your calendar now: mid-January, mid-March and mid-May (or mid-August for lemons)

Frequently Asked Questions

When should you fertilize citrus trees in Arizona?

Oranges and grapefruit: Jan-Feb, Mar-Apr, May-June. Lemons and limes: Jan-Feb, Mar-Apr, Aug-Sept. One-third each time.

Should I fertilize citrus trees in November in Phoenix or Scottsdale?

No. The next feeding window opens in January-February.

How much fertilizer does a citrus tree need?

It depends on size: about 0.25-0.50 lb. of nitrogen for small trees up to 1.75-2.5 lb. for trees 10 feet and taller.

Do you water after fertilizing citrus?

Yes. Work it into the soil and irrigate right away.
