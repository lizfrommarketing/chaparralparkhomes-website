Should I Buy a House With a Pool in Arizona? What Scottsdale and 85250 Buyers Should Weigh

Is buying a house with a pool in Arizona worth it? Barrier law, pump costs, appraisal and resale realities, plus October 2026 Scottsdale market data for 85250 buyers.

Should you buy a house with a pool in Arizona? Yes, if you'll use it and you've priced in the safety rules, the energy and the upkeep. In Scottsdale a pool can be the center of daily life for most of the year, but it's a system that needs a compliant barrier, a working pump and filter, and regular care.

The short version for 85250: treat the pool as part of the inspection and the negotiation, not just the listing photos. Arizona's pool enclosure law sets a five-foot minimum barrier, ENERGY STAR estimates a pool pump can cost up to $310 a year to run, and in today's slower market you have room to ask questions.

The Case For and Against a Pool

Why buyers want one

  • Climate: a pool extends outdoor living through long warm seasons
  • Lifestyle: it's where many Scottsdale homes do their entertaining
  • Choice: in 85250, plenty of resale homes already have one, so you can shop for a pool you like instead of building one

What it really costs

  • Energy: ENERGY STAR says a pool pump can be one of a home's largest energy users, costing up to $310 a year
  • Upgrade potential: an ENERGY STAR variable-speed pump can use 60% less energy than an old single-speed model and save up to $210 a year
  • Upkeep: chemicals, filter cleaning, equipment repair and eventually resurfacing
  • Seasonal decisions: whether to heat the pool and how long to run the pump in winter

Arizona's Pool Barrier Rules in Brief

Arizona's statewide enclosure law, A.R.S. 36-1681, applies to pools at least 18 inches deep and wider than eight feet at any point. Key requirements:

  • Barrier height: at least five feet, measured on the outside
  • Openings: nothing a four-inch sphere can pass through
  • Gates: self-closing and self-latching, opening outward from the pool, with the latch at least 54 inches high (or on the pool side with specific clearances)
  • Setback: the barrier at least 20 inches from the water's edge
  • House as part of the barrier: allowed only with an added four-foot barrier, a qualifying motorized safety cover, or self-latching doors and window latches as the statute describes

The statute exempts homes where all residents are at least six and cities with equal or stricter ordinances, so local rules may govern. See our guide to Scottsdale pool fence requirements.

Sellers have a duty too: under subsection E, anyone selling a home with a pool must give the buyer a pool safety notice approved by the Arizona Department of Health Services.

Does a Pool Add Value?

Here's the honest answer: there's no universal number. An appraiser compares your home to recent nearby sales. If similar 85250 homes with pools have sold for more than comparable homes without them, the appraisal can reflect that. If the pool is dated, cracked or running on failing equipment, it can work against the price.

What tends to help a pool at resale:

  • Clear water and clean tile line on every showing day
  • Equipment records: pump, filter and heater ages and service history
  • A compliant barrier that won't trigger repair requests
  • Usable deck and shade, not just water

What the October 2026 Market Means for Pool Shopping

Phoenix REALTORS' October 2026 report for Scottsdale single-family homes shows:

  • Median sales price: $1,225,000, up 5.6% from October 2025
  • Days on market: 75
  • Percent of list price received: 96.8%
  • Inventory: 1,480 homes, a 4.2-month supply

With roughly four months of supply, buyers have time to inspect the pool thoroughly and negotiate if equipment needs replacing. If you're debating timing, read should I buy now or wait.

Your Pool Checklist Before You Buy

  1. Ask what the home inspection covers. Some inspectors include the pool and spa; others refer you to a pool specialist.
  2. Test every gate. Does it close and latch on its own?
  3. Look at equipment labels for the pump, filter and heater.
  4. Ask for service and chemical logs.
  5. Request the pool safety notice the seller must provide.
  6. Plan for winter: see how to winterize a pool in Arizona.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is buying a house with a pool a bad idea in Arizona?

No, if you budget for safety, energy and upkeep. Arizona requires a five-foot barrier with self-closing, self-latching gates unless an exception applies.

Does a pool add value to a house in Scottsdale?

There's no fixed percentage. It depends on comparable sales and the pool's condition.

Is it harder to sell a house with a pool?

It depends on condition and price point. Clean water, current equipment and a compliant barrier help.

What should I inspect on a house with a pool?

The pool, spa, pump, filter, heater and barrier, either through your inspector or a pool specialist.
