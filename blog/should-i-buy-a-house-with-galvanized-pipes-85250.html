Should You Buy a House With Galvanized or Polybutylene Pipes? A Plumbing Guide for 85250's Older Homes

Buying an 85250 home with galvanized or polybutylene pipes? Often fine, with a plumber's inspection and a plan. Plus what Scottsdale's lead inventory shows.

Should you buy a house with galvanized or polybutylene pipes? Often, yes, if you know what you're buying and price it in. Both materials have known weak points: galvanized steel corrodes over time, and polybutylene was the subject of a nationwide class action over leaks. Get a licensed plumber's inspection during your inspection period and use a written estimate to negotiate.

What about lead in Scottsdale? The City of Scottsdale says that so far it hasn't found any service lines containing lead, and its inventory map is searchable by address. The city has also been replacing galvanized service lines for years because they're more likely to fail. That matters in 85250, where much of the housing went up in Scottsdale's postwar building boom.

Why This Comes Up So Often in 85250

The City of Scottsdale's postwar housing study looked at almost 15,000 houses built between 1946 and 1973 in 236 subdivisions. 85250 sits squarely in that era. Villa Monterey's 758 homes, for example, went up from 1961 to 1969. Original plumbing in homes that age may have been replaced once, partly, or not at all. You can't tell from the listing photos.

Galvanized Pipe: What to Know

The Issue

  • Galvanized steel is zinc-coated steel pipe. Over time it can corrode from the inside, narrowing flow.
  • The City of Scottsdale has worked "for many years" to replace galvanized service lines on its side of the meter due to their increased likelihood of failure, and coordinates replacements with homeowners ahead of time.

What to Look For

  • Low water pressure at several fixtures at once
  • Discolored water when a tap first runs
  • Visible gray threaded pipe at the water heater, under sinks or at the hose bibs
  • A seller's disclosure that lists the pipe type. The Arizona SPDS asks about it; see our seller disclosure guide.

Polybutylene Pipe: What to Know

The History

  • A Tennessee appeals court opinion describes how, during the late 1970s and 1980s, Hoechst Celanese and Shell made and marketed a polybutylene plumbing system that allegedly caused property damage.
  • That led to Cox v. Shell Oil, a nationwide class action filed June 13, 1995, which ended in a settlement without either company admitting liability.

What to Look For

  • Flexible gray, white or blue plastic supply lines, often stamped "PB"
  • Crimp fittings at the water heater or under sinks
  • Signs of past leaks in ceilings, cabinets and walls

Your Inspection Plan

Under the Arizona REALTORS resale contract, the inspection period is 10 days unless you negotiate otherwise. Use it well:

  • Book a plumber alongside the general home inspection, not after
  • Ask for a sewer line camera inspection on older homes
  • Get a written repiping estimate, with PEX or copper options
  • Call your insurance agent about the pipe type before the period ends
  • Look up the home on the City's service line map, or request a free inspection or lead test at 480-312-5355
  • Decide: ask for repairs, a credit, a price change, or walk away within your deadline

Public Side vs. Private Side

The City of Scottsdale explains the two halves of a water service line:

  • Public side: water main to the meter, owned by the city
  • Private side: meter to the home, the owner's responsibility
  • To complete its inventory, the city checks three points: both sides of the meter and where the line enters the home
  • The initial inventory was submitted in October 2024; some addresses are still being verified

The city's pipes inside the home are not part of that inventory. That's what your plumber is for.

For Sellers

  • If you've repiped, gather the invoice and permit and list the work in your disclosure
  • If you haven't, a pre-listing plumbing inspection lets you price it in or fix it on your schedule
  • A home warranty won't stand in for repiping; see is a home warranty worth it?

FAQ: Galvanized and Polybutylene Pipes

Should I buy a house with galvanized plumbing?

It can still be a good buy. Get a plumber's inspection and estimate, and negotiate with the findings.

Should I buy a house with polybutylene pipes?

Go in informed. Confirm the pipe type, price a repipe and talk to your insurance agent.

Does Scottsdale have lead pipes?

The city says it hasn't found any lead service lines so far; check your address on its map.

What is the difference between the public and private side of a water service line?

Main to meter is the city's; meter to house is yours.
