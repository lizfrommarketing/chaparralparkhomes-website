How to Lower Property Taxes in AZ: A Scottsdale 85250 Guide to Appeals, Claims and the Dec. 15 Tax Court Deadline

How do you lower property taxes in Arizona? Check your Notice of Value, fix errors, claim primary-residence status and know the Dec. 15, 2026 Tax Court deadline. An 85250 guide.

How do you lower property taxes in Arizona? In Maricopa County, the Assessor lists three routes: appeal your Full Cash Value if it's above market, file a Notice of Claim to correct factual or classification errors from the current or past three tax years, and file a Residential Legal Class Change if the home is your primary residence but isn't classified that way.

What's timely right now? The Assessor-level appeal window for 2027 values closed April 21, 2026, but the Assessor's calendar lists Dec. 15, 2026, as the owner deadline to take a 2027 valuation appeal straight to the Arizona Tax Court (A.R.S. 42-16201). Error claims and primary-residence reclassification have their own rules and don't depend on that window.

Data note: Deadlines, forms and definitions come from the Maricopa County Assessor's Appeals page, its 2026 tax-year calendar and its Notice of Value FAQ. This is general information, not tax or legal advice. No market statistics are included in this post.

The Three Ways to Lower Your Bill

  • Valuation appeal: your Full Cash Value (FCV) doesn't reflect market value
  • Notice of Claim: a factual or classification error, like the wrong square footage, in tax years 2026, 2025, 2024 or 2023
  • Residential Legal Class Change: the home became your primary residence and the record still shows it as a rental or secondary home

First, Read Your Notice of Value

The Two Numbers

  • Full Cash Value (FCV): the Assessor's estimate of market value, and the number you can appeal
  • Limited Property Value (LPV): the number most property taxes are calculated on. Proposition 117 (2012) limits its increase to 5% per year, it can't exceed FCV, and it's not appealable

Why a Lower Value Isn't Always a Lower Bill

The Assessor is clear that your actual tax depends on the levies set by each taxing jurisdiction, and those levies are shared by all taxpayers in the district. If every value drops but a district's budget doesn't, owners can still pay about the same. An appeal helps most when your value is out of line with similar homes.

Timing

Real property notices arrive on or before March 1 each year for the upcoming tax year. The Treasurer issues the actual tax statements in September.

Option 1: Appeal the Value

At the Assessor (Next Spring)

  • Form: Petition for Review of Valuation, AZDOR Form 82130R, or online during appeal season
  • Where: Maricopa County Assessor's Office, 301 W. Jefferson St., Phoenix
  • Emails aren't accepted, and appeals without documentation may be denied
  • Evidence: recent appraisals, comparable sales or proof of legal classification

For the 2027 notices mailed Feb. 20, 2026, the petition deadline was April 21, 2026. The Assessor's calendar set Aug. 15 for Assessor decisions and Oct. 15 for State Board of Equalization decisions.

At Tax Court (Before Dec. 15, 2026)

If you missed the Assessor deadline, or want to skip the administrative process, the Assessor says you can file with the Arizona Tax Court or Small Claims Court for residential property. Property valued over $2 million goes to Superior Court. The Assessor lists Tax Court at 125 W. Washington, Phoenix, 602-506-8297. Court rules and filing fees apply, so check with the court before you file.

Option 2: Fix an Error With a Notice of Claim

Wrong square footage, a phantom pool or the wrong classification? A Notice of Claim covers errors in the current or past three tax years, and if it's approved, the County Treasurer may issue a refund. Include proof: blueprints, permits, ownership records or classification documents.

Option 3: Claim Your Primary Residence

If the home became your primary residence between Jan. 1 and July 1, 2026, the Assessor says you can update the classification for both 2026 and 2027. After July 1, 2026, the change applies to 2027 only.

Documents (Name and Address Must Match)

  • Driver's license, voter registration or the address page of a tax return
  • Plus a vehicle registration or utility bill

New 85250 owners who bought this year should check this one first. It's the easiest to overlook.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I lower my property taxes in Arizona?

Appeal an above-market value, fix errors with a Notice of Claim or reclassify a primary residence.

Is it too late to appeal my 2027 property value?

At the Assessor level, yes. The Assessor's calendar lists Dec. 15, 2026, for a Tax Court filing.

What's the difference between FCV and LPV?

FCV is market value and is appealable. LPV drives most taxes, rises at most 5% a year and isn't appealable.

When are Arizona property tax bills sent?

The Treasurer issues statements in September.
