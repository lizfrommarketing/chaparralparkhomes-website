How to Flush a Water Heater: A Step-by-Step Guide for Scottsdale's Hard Water and 85250 Homes

How to flush a tank water heater step by step, how often to do it with Scottsdale's 13-17 grains-per-gallon water, and when to stop and call a plumber.

How do you flush a water heater? Turn off the gas or power, let the tank cool, shut the cold-water supply, run a garden hose from the drain valve, and flush until the water runs free of sediment. Then close the valve, refill the tank completely and restore the heat. Your owner's manual is the final word for your model.

Why it matters in 85250: Scottsdale water is hard. The city's 2026 Water Quality Report lists approximate hardness of 13 to 17 grains per gallon (about 215 to 290 mg/L), and those minerals settle in the bottom of your tank as sediment. A.O. Smith says most tank heaters need flushing at least once a year, and more often in hard-water areas.

Data note: Hardness figures are from Scottsdale Water's 2026 Water Quality Report and hard water fact sheet. Maintenance guidance is from A.O. Smith and a U.S. Department of Energy Building America fact sheet. Read November 16, 2026. Always follow your manufacturer's instructions.

How Hard Is the Water in Chaparral Park?

Scottsdale's report splits the city into three hardness zones, and Chaparral Road is one of the boundaries:

  • South of Indian School Road: 13-15 grains per gallon (230-260 mg/L)
  • Indian School Road to Chaparral Road: 13-17 gpg (215-285 mg/L)
  • North of Chaparral Road: 13-17 gpg (230-290 mg/L)

The city's fact sheet explains that hard water leaves scale, a coating of calcium carbonate, inside pipes and appliances. Scale isn't a health risk, but it can reduce the efficiency of some appliances. More on that in our guide to Scottsdale's hard water.

How Often to Flush a Water Heater

  • At least once a year for most tank heaters, per A.O. Smith, with more frequent flushing in hard-water areas
  • A quart every three months: a DOE Building America fact sheet lists this as routine maintenance
  • Test the temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve every six months, and inspect the anode rod every three to four years, per the same DOE sheet
  • Your manual wins: some manufacturers specify a different interval for your model

Strange rumbling or popping can point to sediment, though A.O. Smith notes noises can have several causes.

How to Flush a Tank Water Heater, Step by Step

1. Turn off the heat and let it cool

  • Gas: turn the gas control to OFF (some manuals use the pilot setting)
  • Electric: switch off the breaker or disconnect
  • Wait a few hours; A.O. Smith recommends thick gloves and safety goggles

2. Shut the cold-water supply

Close the valve on the cold-water pipe going into the heater.

3. Connect a hose

Attach a garden hose to the drain valve near the bottom and run it to a spot that can handle several gallons of hot water.

4. Let air in and drain

Open a hot-water tap in the house so the tank drains freely, then open the drain valve by hand or with a flathead screwdriver.

5. Flush until it runs clear

Briefly open the cold supply to stir and rinse the bottom, and keep flushing until you no longer see sediment.

6. Refill before restoring heat

Close the drain valve, remove the hose and open the cold supply. Leave the hot taps open until air stops sputtering, then close them. Only then turn the breaker back on or set the gas control to ON, relighting the pilot if your label calls for it.

When to stop: if the valve is clogged, won't close or leaks, A.O. Smith advises calling a plumber or service technician.

Set the Temperature While You're There

Scottsdale's hard water fact sheet suggests setting water heaters to 120-125 degrees, and the DOE fact sheet notes 120°F is enough for washing and bathing. A lower setting also helps slow scale.

What It Means When You Buy or Sell

Sediment and scale are part of what wears a tank out. See how long a water heater lasts in Arizona for warning signs and replacement rules.

  • Sellers: a dated flush log and a clean drain valve show the home was cared for
  • Buyers: look for the manufacture date on the label and ask when the tank was last flushed
  • Inspectors typically note corrosion, leaks and the T&P discharge line

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should you flush a water heater in Scottsdale?

At least once a year, and more often with hard water, per A.O. Smith. Scottsdale water runs about 13-17 grains per gallon.

How do you flush a water heater with a hose?

Heat off, cold supply closed, hose on the drain valve, hot tap open, then flush until clear. Refill fully before restoring heat.

What if my water heater won't drain?

Sediment can clog the valve. Call a plumber rather than forcing it.

Do tankless water heaters need to be flushed?

They need periodic descaling instead. Follow your manual.
