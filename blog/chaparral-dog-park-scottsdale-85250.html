Chaparral Dog Park Scottsdale: Hours, Maintenance Days, Rules and What to Know Before You Go

Chaparral Dog Park in Scottsdale: open 5:30 a.m.-10 p.m., closed Tuesday and Friday mornings for turf care. Rules, licensing and tips for 85250 dog owners.

What are the Chaparral Dog Park hours? The City of Scottsdale lists the dog park at 5401 N. Hayden Road as open 5:30 a.m. to 10 p.m., year-round. It is closed for maintenance every Tuesday and Friday from 8 a.m. to noon, so plan those mornings around a greenbelt walk instead.

Why do so many 85250 neighbors talk about it? The city calls Chaparral "Scottsdale's most popular dog park," with three acres of grass, benches, play features and water fountains. It is split into an Active Dogs area, a Passive Dogs area and a third area that rotates out for turf repair. For anyone living near Chaparral Park, it is often the daily rhythm of the neighborhood.

Data note: Hours, rules and features come from the City of Scottsdale's dog parks and Chaparral Park pages. Licensing details come from Maricopa County Animal Care & Control. No market statistics are included in this post.

Chaparral Dog Park at a Glance

The Basics

  • Address: 5401 N. Hayden Road, Scottsdale, AZ 85250 (inside Chaparral Park)
  • Dog park hours: 5:30 a.m. to 10 p.m., year-round
  • Maintenance closures: Every Tuesday and Friday, 8 a.m. to noon
  • Surface: About three acres of grass
  • Amenities: Benches, play features and water fountains

Three Areas, One Rotation

The city divides the park into three specialized areas:

  • Active Dogs: room for the sprinters and wrestlers
  • Passive Dogs: a calmer space for seniors, small dogs and shy personalities
  • Rotating rest area: closed for maintenance and turf repair while the grass recovers

That rotation is the reason the turf holds up under heavy daily use. If your favorite side is closed, it is resting, not gone.

The Rules Worth Knowing Before Your First Visit

The City of Scottsdale posts these rules for its dog parks:

  • No more than four dogs per visitor
  • An adult must accompany anyone under 16
  • All dogs must be currently licensed and vaccinated
  • Puppies under four months aren't allowed until they've had their license and vaccinations
  • Female dogs in heat aren't permitted in the exercise area
  • Aggressive or antisocial dogs must be removed immediately
  • Bag and dispose of waste using the canisters provided, and fill any hole your dog digs
  • No food or alcohol in the exercise area
  • Dogs should be under voice control
  • Outside the fenced area, leashes are required, no longer than 6 feet (Scottsdale Revised Code, Ch. 20, Sec. 20-32)

The city doesn't supervise the dog park, and visitors use it at their own risk. Dog attacks should be reported to Maricopa County Animal Care & Control at 602-506-7387.

Licensing Your Dog in Maricopa County

New to the area? This is the step people miss. Maricopa County requires a license for every dog 3 months or older, and a current rabies vaccination is required to apply.

  • Spayed or neutered dogs: $22
  • Unaltered dogs: $55
  • Owners 65 or older: $10 per spayed or neutered dog
  • Late fees: $3 a month for altered dogs, $6 a month for unaltered dogs
  • New residents: the county says to purchase a license within 30 days

Keep the tag on your dog's collar. It's your proof of licensing at the park gate and your dog's fastest ride home if it ever wanders.

Local Tips for Chaparral Dog Park

Timing

  • Early mornings and evenings are when the desert is most comfortable for paws. The 10 p.m. closing time makes after-work visits easy, even in summer
  • Tuesdays and Fridays: go before 8 a.m. or after noon
  • Holidays: the Chaparral Park community center lists closures on Veterans Day and Thanksgiving. Those closures are for the community center, so check the city's dog park page for any separate dog park notices

Make a Morning of It

Chaparral Park is a 100-acre city park. Beyond the dog park, the city lists:

  • A lake for boating and fishing with a fishing pier
  • A greenbelt multi-use path
  • An inclusive playground, a pool and a community center
  • Six lighted ball fields, sand volleyball courts and ramadas

Leashed dogs can join you on the path outside the fenced area. See our guide to fishing at Chaparral Lake for the other half of the park.

Other Scottsdale Dog Parks

If Chaparral is in its maintenance window, the city also runs:

  • Vista del Camino Dog Park, 7800 E. Pierce St., a half-acre park with shade trees
  • Horizon Dog Park, 15444 N. 100th St.
  • Thompson Peak Dog Park, 20199 N. 78th Place, with 3.5 acres of grass

Shaping the Park's Future

The City of Scottsdale Parks and Recreation Department has an open Speak Up Scottsdale project inviting the community to "help shape the future of Chaparral Dog Park." If you're a regular, it's worth adding your voice.
