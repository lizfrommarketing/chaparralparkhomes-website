Do Pipes Freeze in Arizona? How Cold It Has to Get and How to Protect a Scottsdale Home

Do pipes freeze in Arizona? Rarely, but Scottsdale averages about 3 nights below 32°F a winter. When to drip faucets, what to wrap, and what to do if a pipe bursts.

Do pipes freeze in Arizona? Occasionally. NOAA's 1991-2020 climate normals for Scottsdale Municipal Airport average about 3 nights per winter below 32°F: roughly 1.4 in December, 1.0 in January, 0.4 in February and 0.2 in November. Most Scottsdale nights stay well above that, but a single cold snap can still crack an exposed line.

How cold does it have to get? The City of Phoenix's plumbing FAQ says outdoor household pipes typically begin to freeze at about 20°F. Scottsdale Water says dripping outside faucets isn't necessary unless it's expected to be 28°F or below for at least 4 hours. In 85250, the parts that freeze first are usually the exposed ones: backflow preventers, hose bibs, pool equipment and drip irrigation.

Data note: Freeze frequency and normal lows come from NOAA NCEI 1991-2020 monthly climate normals for Scottsdale Municipal Airport (station USW00003192). Prevention steps come from Scottsdale Water and the City of Phoenix. No market statistics are included in this post.

What Freezes First in a Scottsdale Home

Most Valley homes have plumbing inside insulated walls or under the slab, which is why freezes rarely cause damage indoors. The risk sits outside:

  • The backflow preventer on your water line or irrigation system
  • Hose bibs, especially with a garden hose still attached
  • Drip irrigation and sprinkler lines, which the City of Phoenix notes can freeze and break
  • Pool and spa equipment, plus exposed plumbing on the pad
  • Pipes along outside walls under sinks

The City of Phoenix says garage pipes feeding a water heater should be protected as long as the garage door stays closed.

How to Protect Your Pipes Before a Freeze

Scottsdale Water's cold-weather checklist:

Outside

  • Wrap exposed pipes with a towel, blanket or small rug (foam or rubber pipe insulation works for longer-term protection)
  • Remove garden hoses from outdoor faucets
  • Cover foundation vents
  • Turn off drip irrigation and sprinklers
  • Set the pool pump to run from 9 p.m. to 7 a.m. "Running water won't freeze"

Inside

  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so warm air can circulate
  • Know where your shut-off valve is. Scottsdale says the property owner's cut-off valve is typically located next to the water meter box

Only on a Hard Freeze

  • Drip outside faucets at about 5 drops per minute for 24 hours, but only when temperatures are expected to be 28°F or below for at least 4 hours

Can Pipes Freeze in One Night?

Yes. An exposed line can freeze in one cold night. That's why the checklist above happens before the cold arrives, not after. Newer homes are generally better insulated, but the backflow preventer and irrigation lines are exposed on any home, new or old.

If a Pipe Freezes or Bursts

Scottsdale Water's guidance:

  • Be patient. Pipes in the sun usually thaw by mid-morning; shaded pipes may take until after midday
  • Never thaw a pipe with a torch or open flame
  • If a pipe bursts: shut off the water at your main shut-off valve, then call a licensed plumber
  • Check your pressure regulator: Scottsdale notes a pressure-reducing valve has been required on new buildings since 1995, and household pressure should stay between 40 and 120 psi

After any burst pipe, photograph the damage and call your insurer. Water claims show up in the five-year insurance history Arizona sellers provide to buyers.

FAQ: Frozen Pipes in Arizona

Do pipes freeze in Arizona?

Occasionally. Scottsdale averages about 3 nights a winter below 32°F.

How cold does it have to be for pipes to freeze?

Typically about 20°F outside, per the City of Phoenix.

Should I drip my faucets in Scottsdale?

Only if it's expected to be 28°F or below for 4+ hours.

What should I do if a pipe freezes or bursts?

No open flame. Shut off the main and call a licensed plumber.
