How to Get a Building Permit in Scottsdale: SPUR Portal, Common Projects and 85250 Tips

How do you get a building permit in Scottsdale? Apply through Scottsdale SPUR for projects after Jan. 6, 2026. Pools, fences, remodels and many trade jobs need permits.

How do you get a building permit in Scottsdale? For nearly all new requests, you apply through Scottsdale SPUR — Smart Planning, Use and Regulations — the city’s online planning and permitting portal. Planning and Development Services launched SPUR in January 2026, and projects initiated after Jan. 6, 2026 are managed there. Create a SPUR account, choose the permit type, upload documents, pay fees, track status and schedule inspections in one place.

What if you skip it? City Permit Services states that work completed without a required permit will be fined an amount equal to double the permit fee. In Chaparral Park and ZIP 85250, that warning shows up most often on pools, patio covers, walls, remodels and trade upgrades that looked “too small to bother.”

Data note: Process and project lists come from City of Scottsdale Planning and Development pages: Permit Services, Home Improvement, Scottsdale SPUR, Sheds/Garages/Pergolas and Swimming Pools. No fee dollar amounts are quoted here unless published on those pages for a specific permit — none are invented.

Permit Path at a Glance

  • Portal: Scottsdale SPUR (required for city approval / permits)
  • Cutoff: projects after Jan. 6, 2026 → SPUR; earlier projects may still use legacy eServices
  • In person: One Stop Shop, 7447 E. Indian School Road (payments also accepted here)
  • Help line: Planning and Development Services 480-312-2500
  • Inspections: Inspection Services 480-312-5750
  • First inspection deadline: within 180 days of issuance or the permit expires (building permits: each succeeding inspection within 180 days of the latest)
  • Working without a permit: fine equal to double the permit fee

What Typically Needs a Permit

Building permits (examples from city lists)

Scottsdale’s Permit Services and Home Improvement pages say building permits are required for construction work such as:

  • Swimming pools or spas
  • Walls / fences
  • Patio covers, patio/garage enclosures, carports, ramadas
  • Accessory buildings and guest homes
  • Remodels and additions (moving walls; adding electrical and/or plumbing)
  • Alteration of building exterior; solar heater installation; sewer repair (as listed)

Pools: the city’s swimming pool page states a pool permit is required for installation and covers related electrical and plumbing. You submit construction and site plans, including required barrier fencing locations and heights. Related reading: Scottsdale pool fence requirements.

Minor remodels / additions: required when moving walls or adding electrical and/or plumbing; single-story under 1,000 sq. ft. uses the city’s Minor Addition / Remodel packet.

Trade permits

  • Electrical: install, alter, reconstruct or repair wiring on buildings, structures, pools or mechanical equipment
  • Mechanical: furnaces, refrigeration or other AC systems (except portable or window units)
  • Plumbing: plumbing or gas systems, lawn sprinkler supply, water heater replacement

Many single-family trade jobs qualify as minimum permits — online, no plan review, compliance at inspection (examples: like-for-like AC swap, water heater replacement, listed electrical service items).

What Usually Does Not Need a Full Building Permit

Cosmetic work without structural change or skilled trades often needs no permit. City examples include replacing non-structural interior finishes, cabinets/countertops, same-material re-roofing and interior painting.

Courtesy plan review (no permit, but city review still required) examples include:

  • Detached, non-habitable structures smaller than 200 sq. ft. with no water, gas, sewer or electrical connections (site plan required)
  • Patio slabs or sidewalks/drives not over 30 inches above grade
  • Walls three feet or less, except retaining walls (site plan required)
  • Low-voltage landscape accent lighting
  • Masonry outdoor fireplaces, fire pits and barbeques

HOA / CC&Rs may still restrict items the city does not permit. Accessory setbacks and rear-yard coverage rules are on the Sheds/Garages/Pergolas page; overlays can be stricter. Related: casita / ADU rules.

How to Apply in Scottsdale SPUR

  1. Open Scottsdale SPUR and create an account
  2. Choose Apply and select the correct permit type (or search keywords in the Application Assistant)
  3. Enter the property, scope description and valuation of the work
  4. Add contacts and upload required documents (requirements vary by scope; follow file rules in the SPUR guide)
  5. Submit, then pay fees through the secure portal or at the One Stop Shop when invoiced
  6. After issuance, schedule inspections in SPUR and watch the 180-day inspection clock

Pay only through the secure portal or in person at the One Stop Shop — never by wiring funds to an email request. Call 480-312-2500 if an invoice looks off.

Chaparral Park Project Notes

Common 85250 SPUR triggers: new pool/spa and barriers, patio covers or enclosures, walls/fences above courtesy height, remodels that move walls or add trades, and casita concepts. Pair timing with construction noise hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you get a building permit in Scottsdale?

Apply in Scottsdale SPUR, upload documents, pay fees and schedule inspections. Projects after Jan. 6, 2026 use SPUR. Help: 480-312-2500.

What home projects need a Scottsdale building permit?

Pools/spas, walls/fences, patio covers, enclosures, accessory buildings, guest homes and many remodels, plus matching electrical, mechanical and plumbing permits. Unpermitted required work: fine equal to double the permit fee.

Do small sheds need a permit in Scottsdale?

Under 200 sq. ft., non-habitable, with no utilities: courtesy plan review (site plan), not a full building permit. Larger or utility-served structures need the proper permit path.

Where do I apply and who do I call?

SPUR online; One Stop Shop 7447 E. Indian School Road; Planning 480-312-2500; Inspections 480-312-5750.
