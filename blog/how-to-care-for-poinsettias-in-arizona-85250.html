How to Care for Poinsettias in Arizona: Light, Water, Frost and Keeping Them Past the Holidays in 85250

Keep poinsettias bright through December in dry Scottsdale air: six hours of indirect light, 65 to 70 degrees, water when the surface is dry, no frost, and how to plant one outside in spring.

How do you care for a poinsettia in Arizona? Keep it indoors in bright, indirect light for at least six hours a day, between about 65 and 70 degrees, and water thoroughly whenever the soil surface feels dry, then drain the excess. Keep it away from heater vents, fireplaces and cold drafts.

The Arizona twist: Our dry winter air and chilly desert nights are the two biggest risks. University of Arizona Cooperative Extension notes that temperatures below 50 degrees cause leaf drop, and frost will usually kill a poinsettia.

Data note: Care guidance is from University of Arizona Cooperative Extension publications (Yavapai County poinsettia care column and Master Gardener handout, the Pima County December gardening guide, and Frost Protection, AZ1002). Pet guidance is from the ASPCA. Read November 23, 2026.

The Indoor Basics

Light

  • At least six hours of bright, natural light daily
  • A south or west window with indirect light is ideal
  • Keep hot afternoon sun off the colorful bracts, which can fade or burn

Temperature

  • Aim for no warmer than 70 degrees by day and no cooler than 65 at night
  • Below 50 degrees brings chilling injury and leaf drop
  • Don't let leaves touch a cold window on a winter night

Water

  • Water thoroughly when the surface feels dry to a light touch
  • Water until it drains from the bottom, then pour off what collects
  • Remove decorative foil when you water. A plant sitting in water can develop root rot
  • Over- and under-watering both cause yellow leaves that drop

Drafts and dry heat

  • Keep it away from heater vents, fireplaces, space heaters and ceiling fans
  • In a dry Scottsdale house with the heat on, check the soil every day or two

Can Poinsettias Live Outside in Scottsdale?

Not through winter cold snaps. UA Extension's Frost Protection guide puts Maricopa County's average first frost between November 21 (Buckeye) and December 12 (central Phoenix), and the average last frost between February 7 (central Phoenix) and April 3 (Mesa). Calm, clear nights are the coldest, with the low near daybreak.

On a covered patio

  • Fine on mild nights, protected from wind
  • Bring pots in when lows head toward 50 degrees or below
  • If a frost warning comes, move them inside rather than covering them

For other plants in your yard, see our guide on how to protect plants from frost in Scottsdale.

Keeping Your Poinsettia After the Holidays

  1. January to frost-free: keep it indoors in bright, indirect light and keep watering
  2. Feed it: UA Extension suggests a balanced, all-purpose houseplant fertilizer after the holidays to encourage new growth
  3. Plant out after the last frost: the Pima County guide recommends planting poinsettias outside in a shady location once all danger of frost has passed
  4. Expect green first: a replanted poinsettia grows as a leafy shrub; holiday color is a bonus, not a promise

Pets and Poinsettias

The ASPCA lists poinsettias as toxic to dogs and cats because the sap irritates the mouth and stomach, sometimes causing vomiting, but calls the plant "generally over-rated in toxicity." Keep it on a shelf or table out of reach. If you're worried, call your veterinarian or ASPCA Animal Poison Control at 888-426-4435 (a fee may apply).

  • Dining tables for a weekend, then back to the window
  • Grouped by a bright west window with sheer curtains
