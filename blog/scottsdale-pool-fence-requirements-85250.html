Scottsdale Pool Fence Requirements: A Guide for 85250 Homeowners

Scottsdale pool fence requirements: 60-inch barriers, self-closing gates that open outward, latches at 54 inches. What 85250 owners and sellers need.

What are Scottsdale's pool fence requirements? For single-family pools, spas and hot tubs built after July 20, 1995, the City of Scottsdale requires a barrier at least 60 inches tall, measured on the side away from the water. Openings can't let a 4-inch sphere pass, there can be no climbable handholds or footholds, and pedestrian gates must open outward, self-close and self-latch, with an exterior latch release at least 54 inches high.

Do you have to have a pool fence in Arizona? Yes, with nuances. State law sets a baseline and lets cities adopt equal or stricter rules. Scottsdale does exactly that, and it's explicit: there are no exemptions for households without children.

Data note: Requirements summarized from the City of Scottsdale pool page, Scottsdale's adopted 2021 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code amendments (Section 305) and A.R.S. § 36-1681. This applies to homes inside Scottsdale city limits. Have the City or a licensed contractor confirm compliance for your specific property.

Why This Matters in 85250 Right Now

Many 85250 homes around Chaparral Park have backyard pools, and plenty date from before the current rules. Fall is when owners re-landscape, add spas or replace fencing. It's also when Scottsdale Water's pool removal rebate has people weighing whether to keep the pool at all. Any of those projects can trigger barrier questions. So can a sale.

The Core Barrier Rules (Scottsdale Code, Section 305)

Height & Clearance

  • 60 inches minimum, measured outside the barrier and for 3 feet out from it (so no planter or ledge "shortens" the fence)
  • Gap at the bottom: no more than 2 inches over grass or gravel, 4 inches over concrete
  • Openings: must not pass a 4-inch sphere
  • Solid walls: no handholds or footholds beyond normal construction tolerances

Fence Styles

  • Horizontal rails less than 45 inches apart: rails go on the pool side, and vertical spacing can be no more than 1-3/4 inches
  • Horizontal rails 45 inches or more apart: vertical spacing up to 4 inches
  • Chain link: openings no larger than 1-3/4 inches
  • Removable mesh fence: at least 20 inches from the water's edge, with sections attached at 45 inches or higher
  • Pool equipment outside the barrier: at least 36 inches from it, so it can't be used as a step

Gates

  • Open outward, away from the pool, and are self-closing and self-latching
  • Must accommodate a locking device; utility gates stay locked
  • Latch release on the outside: at least 54 inches high (unless it's a self-locking keyed, electronic or combination type)
  • Latch release only on the pool side: at least 3 inches below the top of the gate, with no openings over 1/2 inch within 18 inches of the latch

When the House Is Part of the Barrier

If doors or windows open straight onto the pool area, Scottsdale requires one of the following:

  • Alarms on doors and on windows with sills under 48 inches (UL 2017 water-hazard alarms that sound within 7 seconds, for at least 30 seconds, at 85 dBA), or
  • A safety cover listed to ASTM F1346, or
  • Another approved means, such as self-closing, self-latching doors, that gives equal protection

Older Pools, Spas & Hot Tubs

  • Built before July 15, 1992: need only the 54-inch perimeter yard fence and gate rule in effect at that time
  • Built July 15, 1992 to July 20, 1995: current rules, except a 54-inch perimeter height
  • Spas and hot tubs: the City says they require an approved barrier. Under the code, a spa with a lockable ASTM F1346 safety cover is exempt from the fence-detail sections.
  • Everyone: owners must keep gates, latches, alarms and fencing "in safe and good working order at all times." A barrier that isn't maintained is declared a public nuisance.

Buying or Selling a Home With a Pool

  • Sellers must give a pool safety notice. Arizona law requires a person selling, renting or leasing a home with a pool to give the buyer the Arizona Department of Health Services pool safety notice. Scottsdale adds its own written safety-notice requirement.
  • Buyers investigate during inspection. Arizona REALTORS® says its resale contract includes a pool barrier notice in which the buyer agrees to investigate the regulations and pay compliance costs before occupying, unless the parties agree otherwise in writing. Its flier was last updated July 2020, so confirm against your current contract.
  • Short-term rentals carry extra rules: a 60-inch perimeter fence plus a secondary barrier (a pool fence or audible door and window alarms).
  • Building new? A pool permit requires site plans showing barrier location and height. New fences need their own permit, and 60-inch construction fencing must stay up from excavation until the permanent barrier is finished.

FAQ: Scottsdale Pool Fence Questions

Do you have to have a pool fence in Arizona?

State law requires enclosures, with limited exemptions, and lets cities set stricter rules. Scottsdale's code applies inside city limits, with no exemption for households without children.

What are Scottsdale's pool fence requirements?

At least 60 inches tall, no 4-inch openings, no climbable footholds, and gates that open outward, self-close and self-latch.

Do pool gates open in or out?

Out, away from the pool, under both Scottsdale's code and A.R.S. § 36-1681.

Do older Scottsdale pools have to meet today's fence code?

Not retroactively. The rules depend on the build date, but every barrier must be maintained in working order.
