How to Get Rid of Ants in Arizona: Fire Ants, Odorous House Ants and What Actually Works in 85250

Get rid of ants in Arizona by identifying the species, cutting off food, sealing entry points and using baits, not sprays. University of Arizona guidance for 85250 homes.

How do you get rid of ants in Arizona? Identify the ant, remove the food, seal the entry points and use baits instead of sprays. That's the stepwise approach University of Arizona Cooperative Extension recommends. Baits work because worker ants carry the toxicant back to the nest and queen. Sprays kill the ants you can see and leave the colony behind.

Why it's top of mind in 85250 right now: odorous house ants are most active between 70 and 86°F and often invade after a change in the weather, which describes a lot of fall and spring days in Scottsdale. Here's how to handle the common ants in and around Chaparral Park homes.

Data note: Guidance below comes from University of Arizona Cooperative Extension publications (AZ1954, Backyard Gardener #15 and the Yavapai County Master Gardener ant ID sheet) and ASU's Ask A Biologist, read November 9, 2026. Always follow the product label.

Arizona Ant Facts at a Glance

  • Best approach: sanitation → exclusion → baiting (UA Extension)
  • Don't mix methods: spraying while baiting keeps bait from reaching the colony
  • Odorous house ants: 1/8–1/4 inch, blackish-brown, smell like rotten coconut when crushed, active at 70–86°F
  • Fire ants: native southern fire ant and two desert fire ants are common; the red imported fire ant is not established in Arizona
  • Harvester ants: cleared ring of bare soil around a central hole; considered benign in open spaces
  • Carpenter ants: large (1/4–1/2 inch), leave sawdust-like debris, most active after sunset

Step 1: Identify the Ant

Treatment depends on the species, so start there.

Odorous house ants

UA Extension describes them as 1/8 to 1/4 inch, blackish-brown, and smelling like rotten coconut when crushed. Colonies often have multiple queens and nest outside near shrubs or mulch. Indoors they nest in wall voids, near hot-water pipes and heaters and behind paneling. They eat sweets, vegetables, meats and dead insects.

Southern and desert fire ants

According to UA Extension publication AZ1954, southern fire ants nest in bare, sunny soil, often near moisture, and usually don't build mounds. Workers range from 1/8 to over 1/4 inch, and they're most active in the morning and early evening. When disturbed, they swarm, bite and sting. They're common in yards, and the sting hurts.

Harvester ants

Look for a cleared circle of bare soil around a central opening. The Yavapai County Master Gardener sheet calls them benign in open spaces and suggests a labeled bait only where they're a problem.

Carpenter ants

These are large ants, 1/4 to 1/2 inch, that nest in stumps, firewood, fence posts and hollow doors or window frames. Watch for sawdust-like frass outside the nest. UA notes that baits work if the product label lists carpenter ants.

Step 2: Cut Off Food and Water

Sanitation comes first, according to UA Extension:

  • Wipe up spills and crumbs promptly
  • Store food, including pet food, in sealed containers
  • Fix drips under sinks and around irrigation. Fire ants favor soil near moisture
  • Keep firewood and wood piles away from the house to avoid giving carpenter ants a nesting site

Step 3: Follow the Trail and Seal It

Scout ants lay a scent trail from food back to the nest, and the rest of the colony follows it. UA Extension's advice:

  1. Follow the trail to where ants are getting in
  2. Seal cracks and caulk seams at windows, doors, slab edges and utility penetrations
  3. Scrub the trail with detergent to remove the scent markers

Exclusion is hard against an ant this small, so expect to combine it with baiting.

Step 4: Bait, Don't Spray

When prevention isn't enough, UA Extension prefers toxic baits. Products range from boric acid to synthetic insecticides. Workers carry the bait home and share it, which is how the bait reaches the queens.

  • Diets shift between sugars, fats and proteins with colony needs. If ants ignore one bait, try another type
  • Do not use insecticide sprays while baiting
  • Read and follow the label on any product

For persistent or structural infestations, the extension also suggests bringing in knowledgeable professionals.

Step 5: Watch Out for Stings in the Yard

For fire ants in play areas or around the pool deck, shake out shoes and gloves, keep kids and pets away from active nests, and treat the nest area with a product labeled for fire ants. Watch anyone who's been stung for signs of an allergic reaction.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get rid of ants in my house in Arizona?

Identify the ant, remove food, seal entry points, scrub trails and use baits instead of sprays.

Are there fire ants in Arizona?

Yes, native southern and desert fire ants. The red imported fire ant is not established in Arizona, according to UA Extension.

Why do ants suddenly invade my house?

Odorous house ants often invade after a change in the weather and are most active from 70 to 86°F.

Should I spray or bait for ants?

Bait. Sprays keep the bait from reaching the colony, according to UA Extension.
