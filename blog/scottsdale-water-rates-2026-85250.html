Scottsdale Water Rates 2026: Residential Tiers, Meter Fees and What Shows on Your Bill

What are Scottsdale water rates? As of Nov 1, 2026, single-family tiers start at $1.80 per 1,000 gallons; a common 5/8-inch meter base fee is $16.46, plus stormwater and taxes.

What are Scottsdale water rates? For city water customers in Chaparral Park and ZIP 85250, the official answer is on Scottsdale Water’s Water Rates Schedule effective Nov 1, 2026 (City of Scottsdale Revised Code Section 49-48). You pay a monthly base fee based on meter size, plus tiered commodity rates billed per 1,000 gallons.

What does a typical single-family structure look like? On that Nov 1, 2026 sheet, a 5/8-inch meter base fee is $16.46, and single-family residential (and landscape/irrigation by single family) commodity tiers run from $1.80 per 1,000 gallons for the first 5,000 gallons up to $8.41 per 1,000 gallons once use is over 30,000 gallons. This post quotes schedule figures only — not invented household averages.

Data note: All dollar amounts and tier breakpoints come from the City of Scottsdale Water Rates Schedule PDF effective Nov 1, 2026, and the Scottsdale Water Rates and Fees page. Sewer structure notes come from the same Rates and Fees page. No Cromford or MLS statistics are included.

How the Bill Is Built

Scottsdale Water’s Rates and Fees page describes the structure in plain language:

  1. Monthly base service charge — set by water meter size
  2. Monthly quantity / commodity charge — based on how much water you use
  3. Tiered pricing — the unit price rises as volume rises, which the city says creates a pricing incentive for efficient use, especially when treated drinking water is used outdoors at high volumes

Your meter size is not a lifestyle label; it is an infrastructure fact on the account. If you are unsure of meter size, check a recent bill or ask Utility Billing rather than guessing from a neighbor’s total.

Other Account Classes and Surcharges

The Nov 1, 2026 schedule also publishes four-tier commodity tables for multi-family / commercial / industrial / non-residential accounts (and landscape/irrigation by those classes), with rates of $1.80 / $3.39 / $4.96 / $6.55 and an over 20,000 top tier. Outside city / County Island customers may see surcharges of up to 15% on base and commodity rates and up to 25% on miscellaneous service fees. Match your bill’s account class to the correct table, and confirm any surcharge with Utility Billing.

Sewer Charges (Related Structure)

Many accounts share water and sewer on one bill, but the math differs. Scottsdale Water states sewer includes a meter-size base fee plus a volumetric charge using 90% of average use in the prior December–February, recalculated each July. Abnormal winter use (leak or pool refill) may support an adjustment request — forms before June 20 are reviewed ahead of the July update. This post does not invent sample sewer totals.

No Official “Average Bill” on the Rate Sheet

Your total depends on meter size, gallons through the tiers, the $8.10 stormwater charge, the $0.0065/1,000-gallon state fee, taxes, any outside-city surcharge and sewer lines. In 85250, irrigation style and pools are common swing factors. Related: does Scottsdale have hard water, Arizona winter watering schedule.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are Scottsdale water rates?

A meter-based monthly base fee plus tiered rates per 1,000 gallons. Effective Nov 1, 2026, single-family Tier 1 is $1.80 (0–5,000 gallons) and a 5/8-inch base fee is $16.46, per the city’s schedule (SRC §49-48).

Did Scottsdale water rates increase in 2026?

Use the Nov 1, 2026 official schedule as the current published sheet. Compare prior bills to that PDF for any line-item change rather than relying on unofficial averages.

What else appears on a Scottsdale water bill?

$8.10 stormwater account charge, $0.0065 per 1,000 gallons state water quality assurance fee, plus applicable taxes; outside-city/County Island accounts may see surcharges.

How does Scottsdale’s tiered water pricing work?

Base fee by meter size, then a higher unit price as monthly gallons climb through published tiers — a structure Scottsdale Water describes as an efficiency incentive for higher-volume use.
