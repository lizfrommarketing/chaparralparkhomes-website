How Often Should You Clean Your Dryer Vent? A Fire-Safety Guide for Scottsdale and 85250 Homes

Clean the lint filter every load and the dryer vent at least once a year. USFA data, code basics for duct length and roof vents, and warning signs for 85250 homes.

How often should you clean your dryer vent? At least once a year, and sooner if clothes start taking longer to dry. That's the National Fire Protection Association's guidance, along with cleaning the lint filter before or after every load. It matters: the U.S. Fire Administration found failure to clean was the leading factor behind residential clothes dryer fires.

For 85250 homeowners: clean the filter every load, clean or have someone clean the full duct yearly, and check that the outside flap opens. If your vent runs a long way or exits through the roof, put a professional cleaning on the calendar.

Data note: Fire statistics are from the U.S. Fire Administration's report Clothes Dryer Fires in Residential Buildings (2008-2010), based on national NFIRS data. Safety tips are from USFA and NFPA. Venting guidance is from the U.S. Department of Energy's Building America Solution Center (PNNL), which summarizes the International Residential Code. Scottsdale code adoption is from the City of Scottsdale. Read November 11, 2026.

Dryer Fires by the Numbers (USFA)

Each year in the 2008-2010 data, U.S. fire departments responded to an estimated:

  • 2,900 clothes dryer fires in residential buildings
  • 5 deaths and 100 injuries
  • $35 million in property loss

What caused them:

  • Failure to clean: 34%, the leading contributing factor
  • Dust, fiber and lint: 28% of items first ignited
  • Clothing not on a person: 27%

Seasonal pattern: incidents were higher in fall and winter, peaking in January at 11%. That makes November a good month to put this on your list.

Your Dryer Vent Cleaning Schedule

Every load

  • Clean the lint filter before or after each cycle (NFPA, USFA)
  • Never run the dryer without the lint filter in place

Every few months

  • Pull the dryer out and clear lint from behind and around it (USFA)
  • Look at the transition duct for kinks, crushing or tears
  • Run the dryer and confirm the outdoor flap opens

At least once a year

  • Clean lint out of the full vent pipe, yourself or with a dryer lint removal service (NFPA)
  • Clean sooner if drying times creep up

Warning Signs of a Clogged Vent

  • Clothes need a second cycle to dry
  • The dryer runs unusually hot (USFA notes lint buildup can make a dryer overheat)
  • The outdoor flap stays shut or barely moves while the dryer runs
  • Visible lint around the exterior vent hood
  • A crushed or sagging foil or vinyl hose behind the machine

Code Basics: What a Good Dryer Vent Looks Like

Scottsdale's residential code is based on the 2021 International Residential Code, with city amendments effective Jan. 7, 2023. DOE's Building America Solution Center summarizes the IRC dryer-exhaust rules:

  • Duct: rigid or semi-rigid metal, 4 inches in diameter, smooth inside
  • Length: no more than 35 feet, minus 2.5 feet for each 45-degree bend and 5 feet for each 90-degree bend, unless the manufacturer specifies otherwise
  • Transition duct (dryer to wall): up to 8 feet, listed to UL 2158A
  • Joints: clamps or foil tape, no screws poking into the duct to catch lint
  • Exterior end: at least 3 feet from doors and windows, with a damper and no screen
  • Avoid: vinyl, nylon or foil ducts, which DOE notes will void the warranty of most dryers

Moisture matters, too: DOE notes a dryer vents about a gallon of water per typical load, so a disconnected duct dumping into an attic or wall cavity can cause real damage.

Roof Vents and Older 85250 Homes

DOE's guidance is to avoid roof terminations when possible and run the duct horizontally with a slight downward slope. Some homes vent through the roof anyway, often because the laundry sits in the middle of the house. If yours does:

  • Have a professional clean it; roof work in Arizona heat or on tile is not a casual DIY job
  • Ask them to check the roof cap for a screen or a stuck flap
  • Consider whether a shorter, wall-exit route is possible during a remodel (Scottsdale permits apply; see our building permit guide)

Why Buyers and Inspectors Notice

DOE's guidance notes that inspections often reveal crushed, kinked or clogged ducts. A clean vent and a metal transition duct are small, inexpensive fixes that keep the item off a buyer's repair list. Pair this with your other appliance checks, like how long a water heater lasts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should you clean your dryer vent?

At least once a year, and the lint filter every load, per NFPA.

What happens if you don't clean your dryer vent?

Lint restricts airflow and can ignite. Failure to clean was the top factor in residential dryer fires (34%).

How do you know if your dryer vent is clogged?

Longer drying times, a dryer running hot and an outdoor flap that doesn't open.

Can a dryer vent go through the roof?

It can, but DOE advises avoiding roof exits when possible. They're harder to clean.
