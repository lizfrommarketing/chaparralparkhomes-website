How to Prepare for a Home Inspection as a Seller: A Scottsdale 85250 Checklist Based on Arizona's Inspection Standards

Arizona inspectors don't have to move your stuff or relight equipment that's off. Here's how 85250 sellers prepare: utilities on, access clear, paperwork ready, and the 10-day window.

How do you prepare for a home inspection as a seller? Keep all utilities on, clear a path to the electrical panel, water heater, HVAC equipment and attic hatch, fix the small stuff like burned-out bulbs, and leave your repair records out. Arizona's inspection standards say inspectors are not required to move your belongings or operate systems that are shut down, so anything blocked or off can show up in the report as "not inspected."

Why it matters in 85250 right now: Phoenix REALTORS reports homes took a median of 89 days to sell in October 2026, with sellers receiving 96.8% of list price. With buyers taking their time, a clean inspection report helps keep the deal and your price together.

What the Inspector Will (and Won't) Do

Under the Arizona standards, inspectors observe readily accessible systems: structure, exterior, roof, plumbing, electrical, heating, cooling, interiors, insulation and ventilation.

They are not required to:

  • Move personal items, furniture, plants or debris that block access or view
  • Operate a system that's shut down or doesn't respond to normal controls
  • Enter an attic or crawl space if access is blocked or the opening is smaller than about 16 by 24 inches
  • Test smoke or carbon monoxide alarms, or check solar systems

"Readily accessible" means visible without moving personal property. If the panel is behind boxes, it may be listed as not inspected, and the buyer's agent will likely ask for a return visit.

Your Seller Checklist

Two weeks out

  • Service the HVAC and replace filters
  • Fix the little things: dripping faucets, loose handles, running toilets, sticky doors
  • Swap dead bulbs so no one writes "light inoperable, possible wiring issue"
  • Gather paperwork: roof, AC, water heater and pool equipment receipts, permits and warranties

The day before

  • Clear access to the electrical panel, water heater, AC handler, attic hatch, garage, and under every sink
  • Unlock gates, side yards, sheds and the pool equipment area
  • Run the dishwasher and laundry once to confirm they work
  • Leave remotes for the garage door and ceiling fans

Inspection day

  • All utilities on. The Phoenix REALTORS contract says the seller keeps all utilities on, including propane, until close of escrow
  • Pilot lights lit on gas fireplaces and water heaters. Equipment that's off can be treated as shut down, which inspectors aren't required to operate
  • Pets out of the house, and plan to be away for a few hours

The Arizona Timeline

  • Inspection period: the Phoenix REALTORS contract offers 10 days (residential) from contract execution unless you agree to another period
  • Who pays: the buyer pays for property inspections during that period
  • Access: the seller makes the home available for inspections and walk-throughs on reasonable notice
  • After the report: the buyer can send a notice of items they disapprove. Your response matters, so talk with your agent before you answer

Chaparral Park Homes: Common Items to Check

Many 85250 homes have a few decades behind them. Before the inspector arrives, look at:

  • Roof wear and flashing around rooftop AC units
  • Electrical panels and older wiring updates
  • Plumbing supply lines, water heater age and signs of hard-water scale
  • Pool equipment, barriers and gates
  • Stucco cracks and drainage near the foundation

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I prepare my house for a home inspection as a seller?

Keep utilities on, clear access to key equipment, fix small issues and leave out records.

What do home inspectors look for when you're selling?

Readily accessible structure, roof, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, interiors, insulation and ventilation.

Should the seller be home during the inspection?

Usually not. Give buyers space and let your agent handle questions.

How long does the buyer have to inspect in Arizona?

The standard contract provides 10 days for residential, unless the parties choose otherwise.
