How to Deep Fry a Turkey Safely: USDA Steps, Fire Department Warnings and What to Do With the Oil in 85250

Frying a turkey this Thanksgiving? USDA's size, thaw and oil-temperature rules, CPSC's COOK checklist, NFPA's warning, and how to get rid of the oil without clogging your 85250 drains.

How do you deep fry a turkey safely? Use a completely thawed, unstuffed turkey of 12 pounds or less, fry it outdoors well away from the house, heat the oil to 350°F, lower the bird slowly and never leave the fryer unattended. Those are the USDA's rules, and they exist because hot oil and a wet or frozen bird are a dangerous combination.

Is it worth the risk? The National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) strongly discourages oil-based turkey fryers altogether and suggests an oil-free infrared, air or electric fryer, or buying a fried turkey. If you're frying anyway, this guide walks through doing it the careful way, with Thanksgiving on Thursday, November 26.

Data note: Frying steps are from USDA FSIS "Turkey: Alternate Routes to the Table" and "Deep Fat Frying and Food Safety." Fire figures are from NFPA. The COOK checklist is from CPSC's November 19, 2025 holiday release. Drain guidance is from Scottsdale Water. Read November 20, 2026.

Why Thanksgiving Is a Fire-Department Holiday

  • Peak day: NFPA says Thanksgiving is the top day of the year for U.S. home cooking fires, with an estimated 1,139 reported in 2024, more than three times the daily average
  • Cooking is the cause: from 2020 to 2024, 80% of Thanksgiving structure fires involved cooking equipment
  • Runners-up: Christmas Day and Christmas Eve rank second and third
  • Biggest factor: unattended cooking

A turkey fryer combines several gallons of oil, an open flame and a large, heavy, sometimes still-icy bird. That's why the rules below are strict.

Before Fryer Day: Size, Thaw and Measure

Choose the right turkey

USDA says deep frying works for whole turkeys 8 to 12 pounds that are completely thawed and not stuffed. Larger birds are better roasted, or split into parts.

Thaw fully and dry it

Ice and water make hot oil splatter violently. USDA explains that water hitting oil at about 350°F flashes into superheated steam, which throws oil. Pat the turkey dry inside and out.

Measure the oil with water first

USDA's test prevents the most common disaster, an overflowing pot:

  • Place the turkey in the empty pot and add water until it covers the bird
  • Remove the turkey and measure the water
  • That's the amount of oil to use; the oil should cover the turkey by 1 to 2 inches
  • Dry the pot completely before adding oil

Pick a high smoke-point oil

USDA lists peanut, safflower and soybean oil at about 450°F, canola about 435°F, and corn, olive and sunflower about 410°F.

Fryer Day: The Safe Setup

CPSC's checklist spells it COOK:

  • Carefully follow the manufacturer's instructions
  • Overheat: never overheat the oil
  • Overfill: never overfill the fryer
  • Keep the fryer out of the garage, off the deck and away from the house

In 85250 that means no frying under a covered patio, beside a wood fence or on a pergola-shaded deck. Set up on level concrete or pavers in open air, with a clear path around the fryer and children and pets kept well back. NFPA recommends at least three feet of clearance around cooking areas for kids and pets.

Frying, step by step (USDA)

  1. Heat the oil to 350°F and check it with a thermometer
  2. Turn off the burner, then lower the turkey slowly into the oil
  3. Relight the burner if needed and monitor oil temperature the entire time
  4. Cook about 3 to 5 minutes per pound
  5. Lift the turkey out, drain the cavity and check 165°F in the innermost thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast
  6. Rest about 20 minutes before carving

If something goes wrong

  • Never use water on a grease fire, USDA says; it makes burning oil splatter and spread
  • Keep a fire extinguisher nearby; USDA highly recommends it
  • If a fire grows, get everyone away and call 911

After Dinner: What to Do With the Oil

This is where many Scottsdale homeowners create a second problem.

  • Let it cool: USDA suggests about two hours before handling or cleaning the fryer
  • Reuse it: strain, cover and use within a month, per USDA
  • Toss it: pour cooled oil into a sealable container and put it in the trash
  • Never down the sink: USDA warns oil congeals in drains, and Scottsdale Water says grease cools and hardens further down your pipes

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you deep fry a turkey safely?

Thawed, unstuffed, 12 pounds or less, outdoors, oil at 350°F, lowered slowly and never left alone.

How much oil do I need to deep fry a turkey?

Use USDA's water test: the water that covers the turkey equals the oil you need.

How long does it take to deep fry a turkey?

About 3 to 5 minutes per pound at 350°F; confirm 165°F with a thermometer.

How do you dispose of turkey fryer oil?

Cool it, seal it in a container and trash it, or strain and reuse it. Never pour it down a drain.
