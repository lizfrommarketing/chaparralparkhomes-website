How to Stage Your House to Sell in Scottsdale 85250: What the Data Says Buyers Notice First

How do you stage a house to sell? Start with the living room, primary bedroom and kitchen, and stage for the photos. NAR's 2025 staging data, costs and an 85250 checklist.

How do you stage a house to sell? Start with the three rooms buyers' agents rank highest, the living room (37%), the primary bedroom (34%) and the kitchen (23%), according to the National Association of REALTORS® 2025 Profile of Home Staging. Then stage everything for the camera, because listing photos were the presentation tool agents rated most important.

Is it worth it? NAR found that 83% of buyers' agents say staging makes it easier for buyers to picture the home as their own. A typical professional staging bill had a median of $1,500. For many 85250 sellers, a few focused rooms are enough.

Data note: All staging statistics come from NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Staging, based on a February 2025 survey of REALTORS® with 1,266 usable responses. These are national figures, not Scottsdale-specific. No local market statistics are included in this post.

Home Staging by the Numbers

  • 83% of buyers' agents: staging makes it easier to visualize the home as a future home
  • Most important rooms to stage (buyers' agents): living room 37%, primary bedroom 34%, kitchen 23%
  • Most commonly staged (sellers' agents): living room 91%, primary bedroom 83%, dining room 69%
  • Median cost: $1,500 with a staging service; $500 when the seller's agent staged personally
  • Offers: 17% of buyers' agents and 19% of sellers' agents saw a 1% to 5% increase in the dollar value offered
  • Time on market: 30% of sellers' agents saw slight decreases when a home was staged

Step 1: Stage the Rooms That Count

Living Room

This is where buyers decide how a home feels. Pull furniture off the walls into a conversation group, remove extra pieces so the walkways are wide and let the windows do the work. In a ranch-style 85250 home, that often means opening the view to the backyard and pool.

Primary Bedroom

Think calm and hotel-simple: a made bed with neutral bedding, matching nightstands and lamps, and nothing stored under the bed or on the dresser.

Kitchen

Clear the counters almost completely. Leave one or two intentional items, a bowl of citrus or a cutting board, and pack away small appliances, magnets and paperwork.

Step 2: Declutter and Depersonalize

  • Pack a third of what's in every closet and cabinet; buyers open doors
  • Remove family photos and personal collections so buyers can picture their own life
  • Give every room one job: a "bonus room" becomes an office or guest room
  • Store the extras offsite or in the garage, neatly

Step 3: Stage for the Photos

Among buyers' agents, NAR found photos (73%), traditional physical staging (57%), videos (48%) and virtual tours (43%) were much more important or more important to their clients. Sellers' agents put photos even higher, at 88%.

Photo-Day Checklist

  • Every light on, bulbs matching in color
  • Blinds open to the same height
  • Toilet lids down, towels folded, cords hidden
  • Cars out of the driveway, trash cans out of sight
  • Pool clean and blue, patio furniture set

Step 4: Outside, Arizona Style

Curb appeal in Scottsdale is about clean lines and fresh desert landscaping: rake the granite, trim the shrubs off the walls and windows, add a pot or two of seasonal color by the door and make the backyard feel like an outdoor room. Fall and winter are a good time to show off a backyard here, because buyers can actually picture sitting outside.

Step 5: Decide What to Hire Out

  • DIY or agent-assisted: good for move-in-ready homes that need editing, not furniture
  • Partial staging: rent pieces for the living room and primary bedroom only
  • Full staging: for vacant homes, where empty rooms read smaller in photos
  • Virtual staging: useful online, as long as photos are clearly labeled and the home matches what buyers will see
