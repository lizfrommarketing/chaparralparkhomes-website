How to Get Your House Ready for an Appraisal: What Appraisers Look For in Scottsdale and 85250

How to get your house ready for an appraisal: fix safety items, document upgrades, clear access to the attic and systems, and share comparable sales the right way.

How do you get your house ready for an appraisal? Fix anything that affects safety or soundness, tidy up visible wear, document every upgrade, and make the whole house easy to see and measure. The appraiser rates condition and quality from what they can observe, so your job is to make the good things obvious and leave nothing that looks like a defect.

Why it matters right now: Scottsdale's median single-family sale price was $1,225,000 in October 2026, and sellers received 96.8% of list price on average, per Phoenix REALTORS. At those prices, a small gap between contract price and appraised value can mean a large renegotiation, whether you're selling or refinancing.

What an Appraiser Actually Rates

Fannie Mae's guide asks appraisers to rate condition C1 (new) to C6 and to judge the house holistically, on its own merits rather than against the neighbors:

  • C3: well maintained, limited wear, some components updated
  • C4: minor deferred maintenance, adequately maintained, needs only minimal repairs
  • C5: obvious deferred maintenance, some significant repairs needed
  • C6: deficiencies that affect safety, soundness or structural integrity

A C6 anywhere makes the whole home a C6, and the appraisal must be completed "subject to" repairs. Appraisers also classify kitchens and baths as Not Updated, Updated or Remodeled.

The Pre-Appraisal Checklist

Fix the items that get written up

Fannie Mae lists examples of minor conditions that must be reported: worn floor finishes or carpet, minor plumbing leaks, holes in window screens, missing handrails and cracked window glass. Most are inexpensive fixes.

  • Replace cracked panes and torn screens
  • Reattach or install handrails
  • Fix drips under sinks and at hose bibs
  • Make sure smoke and CO detectors work

Clear access

  • Attic: HUD's FHA handbook says the appraiser must observe attic interiors, so clear the hatch
  • Electrical panel, water heater and HVAC: reachable and unlocked
  • Gates and casitas: unlocked, pets secured

Mind the paint on older homes

For FHA loans, the appraiser must report defective exterior paint and require repair of any that exposes the subsurface; homes built on or before Dec. 31, 1978 fall under lead-based paint rules. If your home predates 1979, touch up peeling trim early and ask your agent about lead-based paint rules.

Make the space easy to see

  • Declutter so rooms read at their true size
  • Turn on lights, open blinds
  • Mow, trim and clear the yard; remove anything stored against walls

Document What the Appraiser Can't See

Upgrades hidden in walls and roofs only count if the appraiser knows about them. Prepare a one-page list with dates, costs and permits:

  • Roof, HVAC, water heater and windows
  • Kitchen and bath remodels, with photos
  • Pool equipment, solar ownership status
  • Permits from the city; see how to get a building permit in Scottsdale

Sharing Comparable Sales the Right Way

Under 12 CFR 1026.42, it's permitted to ask an appraiser to consider additional, appropriate property information, including information about comparable properties. Pressuring an appraiser toward a value is not. I prepare a short packet of recent nearby sales with notes on how each compares, and the appraiser decides what to use.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get my house ready for an appraisal?

Fix safety items and visible wear, clear access to systems, document upgrades and declutter.

What hurts a home appraisal?

Safety or structural deficiencies matter most; they require repairs. Visible deferred maintenance is also reported.

Can I give the appraiser comparable sales?

Yes, Regulation Z permits sharing appropriate property information, including comps. Pressure is not allowed.

Do I get a copy of the appraisal?

Borrowers do, promptly upon completion or three business days before closing, whichever is earlier.
