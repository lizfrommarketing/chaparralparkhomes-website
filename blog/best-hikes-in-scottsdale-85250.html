Best Hikes in Scottsdale: Easy to Extremely Difficult Trails Within Reach of 85250 This Winter

The best hikes in Scottsdale this winter, from the Indian Bend Wash path to Pinnacle Peak, the McDowell Sonoran Preserve and Camelback, with current closures.

What are the best hikes in Scottsdale? For an easy walk, start on the 11-mile Indian Bend Wash Greenbelt path; for desert trails, head to the McDowell Sonoran Preserve or Pinnacle Peak Park; for a serious climb, Camelback Mountain's Echo Canyon. All four are a short drive or a walk from Chaparral Park.

Why now: November through spring is prime hiking season, and the city trail pages carry several current closures worth checking before you go, including a section of Tom's Thumb Trail and scheduled parking work at Pinnacle Peak.

Data note: Trail distances, rules and closures are from the City of Scottsdale (McDowell Sonoran Preserve, Pinnacle Peak Park and Indian Bend Wash Greenbelt pages), the McDowell Sonoran Conservancy, and the City of Phoenix Camelback Mountain pages. Read November 17, 2026.

Best Hikes in Scottsdale at a Glance

  • Indian Bend Wash Greenbelt (Easy): 11 miles, 24+ grade-separated crossings
  • Gateway / Bajada Nature Trail (Easy): accessible trail with restrooms, shade and water
  • Pinnacle Peak Park (steady climb): 2 miles one way, 1,300 ft cumulative gain
  • Tom's Thumb (steep): loose gravel, about 1,200 ft gain
  • Echo Canyon at Camelback (Extremely Difficult): 1.14 miles, 1,400 ft elevation change

Easy: The Indian Bend Wash Greenbelt

You don't need a car for this one. The City of Scottsdale describes the greenbelt as parks, lakes, paths and golf courses traversing 11 miles through the heart of Scottsdale, with more than 24 grade-separated crossings so you can avoid major cross traffic.

  • From 85250: the path runs right past Chaparral Park
  • Good for: a morning walk, a jog or an easy bike ride
  • More: our guide to the Indian Bend Wash Greenbelt

Easy to Difficult: The McDowell Sonoran Preserve

The city says the Preserve covers more than 30,500 acres with over 230 miles of multi-use trails, protected since 1994.

Gateway Trailhead

  • Address: 18333 N. Thompson Peak Pkwy., Scottsdale 85255
  • Amenities: parking, restrooms, shade ramadas, water and the accessible Bajada Nature Trail
  • Why go: the Conservancy calls it fully barrier-free, with the Gateway Loop and more challenging interior trails

Lost Dog Wash Trailhead

  • Address: 12601 N. 124th St., Scottsdale 85259
  • Amenities: parking, restrooms, shade, water and an accessible nature trail
  • Why go: one of the closer Preserve trailheads to south Scottsdale

Tom's Thumb (check closures first)

  • The trail: the Conservancy describes loose gravel, very steep climbs and about 1,200 feet of elevation gain
  • Current alert: the city has closed the section between markers TT2 and EE1 due to a large boulder and unstable soil after recent rain, and warns the trailhead may close temporarily during repairs

Preserve rules: dogs must be on a handheld leash no longer than six feet, and e-bikes are prohibited on Preserve trails.

A Steady Climb: Pinnacle Peak Park

  • Address: 26802 N. 102nd Way, Scottsdale 85262
  • Trail: 2 miles one way, 1,300 feet of cumulative elevation gain, high point 2,889 feet
  • Rules: no dogs or bikes on the trail (both are allowed at nearby Brown's Ranch Trailhead)
  • This week: the city lists parking lot closures Nov. 16-20, 2026 for tree trimming and fire mitigation; call the park office at 480-312-0990 with questions

Extremely Difficult: Camelback Mountain

Camelback sits in Phoenix, but it's a quick drive from 85250.

  • Echo Canyon Trail: 1.14 miles, 1,400 feet of elevation change, rated Extremely Difficult, with handrails in steep sections and very little shade
  • Easier option: Bobby's Rock Trail, a 0.2-mile loop with 200 feet of gain from the same trailhead
  • Trailhead: 4925 E. McDonald Dr., open sunrise to sunset
  • Parking: the city says in winter and spring the lot is typically full from late morning through early afternoon on weekdays and all day on weekends
  • Dogs: prohibited on all Camelback trails
  • Second route: the Cholla Trailhead entry is off Invergordon Road

Also note: Phoenix says North Mountain Park will close Dec. 1, 2026 through early 2028 for renovations; other Phoenix Mountains Preserve trailheads stay open.

Smart Winter Hiking Habits

  • Start early for parking, especially at Echo Canyon
  • Carry water, a phone and a map, and wear sturdy shoes, as the city advises at Pinnacle Peak
  • Check the trail page the morning of for closures after storms
  • Watch for rattlesnakes, whose activity varies seasonally

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the easiest hike in Scottsdale?

The Indian Bend Wash Greenbelt path, or the accessible Bajada Nature Trail at Gateway.

How long is the Camelback Mountain hike?

Echo Canyon is 1.14 miles with 1,400 feet of elevation change, rated Extremely Difficult.

How hard is the Pinnacle Peak hike?

2 miles one way with 1,300 feet of cumulative gain. No dogs or bikes.

Can you bring dogs on Scottsdale hiking trails?

Yes in the Preserve, on a six-foot handheld leash. Not at Pinnacle Peak or Camelback.
