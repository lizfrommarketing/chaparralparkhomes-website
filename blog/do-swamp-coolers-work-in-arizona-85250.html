Do Swamp Coolers Work in Arizona? What Scottsdale 85250 Homeowners Should Know Before Summer

Do swamp coolers work in Arizona? Yes, in dry heat, using about a quarter of central AC's energy. Monsoon humidity cuts their punch. Costs, upkeep and winterizing for 85250.

Do swamp coolers work in Arizona? Yes, in dry heat they work well. The U.S. Department of Energy's Building America Solution Center says an evaporative cooler can drop incoming air by 15°F to 40°F, while using about one-fourth the energy of central air conditioning. Their weak spot is humidity, and in Arizona that means the monsoon, which the National Weather Service defines as June 15 through Sept. 30.

Is one worth it? For many Valley homes, a swamp cooler is a low-cost supplement to refrigerated air, not a full replacement: excellent in May and June, less helpful on a sticky August afternoon. And if you have one now, November is the month to winterize it.

Data note: Performance, energy, sizing and maintenance figures come from the Building America Solution Center's Evaporative Cooling Systems guide, run by Pacific Northwest National Laboratory for the U.S. Department of Energy. Monsoon dates come from the National Weather Service. No market statistics are included in this post.

Swamp Coolers at a Glance

  • How they cool: outside air is pulled through wet pads, and evaporation lowers its temperature
  • Temperature drop: 15°F to 40°F, depending on humidity
  • Energy: about one-fourth of central air conditioning
  • Cost: less than half the price to install and operate, before water costs
  • Best conditions: low outdoor humidity; areas with design wet-bulb temperatures of 70°F or lower
  • Tradeoffs: adds humidity indoors, uses water, needs twice-a-year maintenance

Why Humidity Is the Whole Story

An evaporative cooler can only cool air down toward the wet-bulb temperature, the lowest temperature evaporation can reach. The drier the air, the bigger the gap, and the colder the air coming out of the vents.

The Solution Center gives two examples for a typical aspen-pad cooler:

  • 100°F at 10% humidity: supply air of about 73°F, a 27-degree drop
  • 95°F at 50% humidity: supply air of about 84°F, only an 11-degree drop

That's the Arizona pattern in two lines. Late spring and early summer are dry, and a cooler shines. During the monsoon, dew points climb and the same machine struggles. That's why so many Scottsdale homes that still have a cooler also have central AC.

Do Swamp Coolers Work With Windows Closed?

No. An evaporative cooler doesn't recirculate air the way an air conditioner does. It blows fresh outside air through the house, enough to replace the indoor air every 2 to 3 minutes, and that air needs somewhere to go.

Giving the Air an Exit

  • Crack windows in the rooms you want to cool; the air flows toward open outlets
  • Or install ceiling relief vents with dampers that open when the cooler runs (this requires a vented attic)
  • Plan on about 2 square feet of unrestricted relief area per 1,000 CFM of airflow

Sizing

Coolers are rated in CFM, not tons. Common residential sizes run 3,000 to 6,500 CFM, and manufacturers typically suggest 20 to 40 air changes per hour, depending on climate.

Roof, Wall or Ground Mount

Traditional swamp coolers sit on the roof and push air down into attic ducts. The Solution Center notes those units can be hard to service and can lead to roof leaks. Wall- or ground-mounted units are easier to maintain and won't cause roof leaks.

Newer single-inlet designs use 8- to 12-inch-thick media, with cooling effectiveness of 75% to 93%. Look for at least two fan speeds and a vent-only mode, which lets the unit act like a whole-house fan on mild days.

Maintenance and Water

Spring Start-Up

  • Replace or clean the pads
  • Check the belt tension
  • Clean and inspect the cabinet

Winter Shut-Down (Now)

  • Shut off the water line
  • Drain and clean the pan
  • Install a winter cover
  • Close any ceiling vent dampers so warm air stays inside

Hard Water

Scottsdale's mineral-heavy water builds up on pads. A bleed-off valve reduces scale but can raise water use 10% to 50%. A dump pump, which empties the pan every 8 to 12 hours of run time, is the more efficient fix. During the season, drain the pan if the cooler will sit idle for more than a few days; standing water breeds mosquitoes and bacteria.

  • Buyers: ask your inspector to check the cooler, its roof penetration and its water line
  • Sellers: winterize, cover it and disclose whether it works, or remove it and patch the roof properly
  • Everyone: a cooler plus refrigerated air gives you a cheaper option for dry months

Frequently Asked Questions

Do swamp coolers work in Arizona?

Yes, in dry heat. They drop air temperature by 15°F to 40°F, but lose punch during the June 15-Sept. 30 monsoon.

Is a swamp cooler worth it?

Often, as a supplement. They use about one-fourth the energy of central AC, but use water and need upkeep.

Do swamp coolers work better with windows open?

Yes. They need an exit for the air: open windows or ceiling relief vents.

How do I winterize a swamp cooler?

Shut off the water, drain and clean the pan, and cover the unit.
