Heat Pump vs. Gas Furnace in Arizona: Which Makes Sense for a Scottsdale Home?

Heat pump or gas furnace in Arizona? With mild winters, a heat pump handles most Scottsdale heating. Compare costs, SRP rebates and the 2026 tax-credit change.

Is a heat pump or a gas furnace better in Arizona? For most Scottsdale homes, a heat pump. Our winters are mild: NOAA's 1991–2020 normals for Scottsdale Airport put the average January low at 43.4°F, with about one night below freezing in a typical January. A heat pump also cools, so one system covers both seasons.

When does a gas furnace still make sense? If your home already has gas service, you like the hotter air a furnace delivers, or you want a dual-fuel setup where gas takes over on the coldest nights. The cost comparison depends on your SRP or APS electric rate versus your Southwest Gas rate, so get both quotes.

Data note: Temperatures are NOAA NCEI 1991–2020 normals for Scottsdale Municipal Airport. Efficiency figures come from the U.S. Department of Energy. Rebate terms are from SRP's Cool Cash program; tax-credit status is from the IRS. No market statistics or invented utility-cost averages are included.

Heat Pump vs. Gas Furnace at a Glance

  • How it heats: a heat pump moves heat from outside air; a furnace burns gas
  • Cooling: heat pump yes; furnace no (needs a separate AC)
  • Efficiency: DOE says heat pumps can cut heating electricity about 65% versus electric resistance heat
  • Scottsdale January normal: high 66.5°F, low 43.4°F
  • SRP rebate: $75–$225 per ton for qualifying systems
  • Federal 25C credit: ended for systems placed in service after Dec. 31, 2025

How Each System Works

Heat Pump

The Department of Energy compares a heat pump to a refrigerator running in reverse: it uses electricity to transfer heat from a cooler space to a warmer one. In summer it moves heat out of the house; in winter it pulls heat in.

  • One outdoor unit handles heating and cooling
  • High-efficiency models also dehumidify well, per DOE
  • Ductless mini-splits suit casitas and additions without ducts

Gas Furnace

A furnace burns natural gas and blows heated air through your ducts. In most Valley homes it is paired with a separate air conditioner, so you maintain two pieces of equipment.

Dual-Fuel (Hybrid)

DOE describes dual-fuel systems as a heat pump handling most heating in milder weather, with a gas furnace taking over when temperatures drop. If your furnace is aging but your gas line is in place, it's a middle path.

Why Heat Pumps Fit the Valley

  • DOE says heat pumps are an efficient alternative to furnaces and ACs for all climates
  • DOE notes that in warmer climate zones, about 60% of homes rely on furnaces and heat pumps are used in 15%–25%, so many homes still have room to switch
  • Homes with existing ductwork usually have the easiest swap
  • Our real HVAC load is summer cooling, so a heat pump replaces equipment you need anyway

Costs, Rebates and the 2026 Tax-Credit Change

Federal Tax Credit: Ended

The IRS says the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (25C) is not allowed for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. If you installed in 2026, don't count on that credit.

SRP Cool Cash Rebate

For SRP customers, qualifying heat pumps and ACs rated 15.2 SEER2 or higher earn:

  • $75 per ton for single-stage systems
  • $150 per ton for multi-stage systems
  • $225 per ton for variable-capacity systems, including inverter mini-splits

Key rules: installed by an Arizona-licensed contractor before April 30, 2027, replacing or supplementing an existing central system, both indoor and outdoor units replaced on split systems, and the application submitted within six months. SRP also notes that the Efficiency Arizona state program may offer heat pump rebates.

Operating Costs

DOE says heating typically makes up about 30% of a home's utility bill on average. In Scottsdale our heating season is short, which is another reason cooling efficiency often drives the decision.

How to Decide for Your Home

  • Furnace and AC both near end of life? A heat pump replaces both
  • AC failing but furnace newer? A heat pump paired with the furnace creates a dual-fuel system
  • Want a smart thermostat? DOE notes programmable thermostats were historically not recommended for heat pumps, but newer heat-pump models make setbacks cost-effective
  • Ask for a Manual J load calculation. SRP requires it for single-stage rebates, and it prevents oversizing

FAQ: Heat Pumps vs. Gas Furnaces in Arizona

Is a heat pump better than a gas furnace in Arizona?

For most Scottsdale homes, yes. Winters are mild and it also cools.

Is there a federal tax credit for heat pumps in 2026?

No. 25C ended for systems placed in service after Dec. 31, 2025.

Does SRP offer a heat pump rebate?

Yes: $75–$225 per ton for qualifying systems.

How much less electricity does a heat pump use?

About 65% less than electric resistance heat, per DOE.
