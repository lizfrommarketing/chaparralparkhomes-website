How Long Does a Water Heater Last in Arizona? Hard Water, Warning Signs and Replacement Rules in 85250

How long does a water heater last in Arizona? About 12 years on average per Scottsdale, 10-15 for tanks per DOE. Hard water, flushing, permits and tankless.

How long does a water heater last in Arizona? The City of Scottsdale's homeowner guide says the average water heater lasts about 12 years, and the U.S. Department of Energy lists 10 to 15 years for a storage tank and about 20 years for a tankless unit. With regular maintenance, the city notes some tanks run two or three times as long.

What shortens that life in 85250? Mostly sediment and corrosion. Scottsdale's 2026 Water Quality Report puts city water at roughly 13 to 17 grains per gallon of hardness, and the city names overheating from sediment buildup as a main cause of tank failure. Once a tank rusts through, it can't be repaired. Replacement is the only fix.

Data note: Lifespan, maintenance and installation details come from the City of Scottsdale's "Citizen's Guide to Water Heaters," its home improvement permit page, its 2026 Water Quality Report, and the U.S. Department of Energy. No market statistics are included in this post.

Water Heater Lifespan at a Glance

  • Average water heater: about 12 years (City of Scottsdale, citing industry statistics)
  • Storage tank: 10 to 15 years (DOE)
  • Tankless (on-demand): about 20 years (DOE)
  • Scottsdale water hardness: about 13 to 17 grains per gallon, or roughly 215 to 290 mg/L
  • What can't be repaired: a rusted-through tank

Why Hard Water Matters for Your Tank

Sediment

Calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of the tank. Sediment insulates the water from the burner or element, and the city lists overheating from sediment as a main cause of failure. For high-mineral areas, Scottsdale recommends:

  • Drain at least 5 gallons from the drain valve every six months
  • Swap a plastic drain valve for a brass ball valve, which has a bigger opening and makes flushing easier
  • Ask a licensed plumber to check the anode and sediment periodically, sometimes as part of an annual service plan

The Anode Rod

Every tank has a sacrificial anode rod, usually magnesium or aluminum, that corrodes so the steel tank doesn't. The city's guide says:

  • Replacing the anode every 3 to 4 years "will add considerable life" at a fraction of replacement cost
  • Replace it more often if your water is softened, because softeners wear anodes faster
  • A rotten-egg smell can come from bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode; an aluminum rod can help

If you already have a softener, see our guide to Scottsdale's hard water. The anode schedule is the trade-off most people never hear about.

Signs Your Water Heater Is Going Out

The city's guide and its descriptions of the main components point to these clues:

  • Leaking around the base: the city says repair is likely necessary and the unit "may be suspect for replacement"
  • Running out of hot water fast, or only lukewarm water: a broken dip tube can cause this
  • No hot water on an electric unit: the high-limit switch may have tripped (a red reset button in the top thermostat)
  • Rotten-egg odor: an anode reaction
  • Age: a unit at or past 12 years deserves a closer look

The city also recommends a visual check at least once a month and keeping the area around the heater clear. Never store gasoline or flammable liquids near it.

Replacing a Water Heater in Scottsdale

Permits

  • Scottsdale lists water heater replacement (gas or electric) as a Minimum Plumbing Permit, along with plumbing for a water softener and a hot water re-circulator
  • No plan review is required; apply online through the city's permit portal
  • The city says work done without a permit is fined double the permit fee
  • A homeowner may pull the permit and do the work, but the city strongly cautions novices, and state law requires a licensed plumbing contractor to warranty the installation

Installation Rules the City Highlights

  • In a garage, pilots, burners or heating elements must sit at least 18 inches above the floor
  • A shut-off valve is required on the cold-water inlet
  • Unions within 12 inches of the heater make future removal easier
  • The T&P drain line, at least 3/4 inch, must end outside, 6 to 24 inches above grade, pointing down
  • Gas units are prohibited in bedrooms and bathrooms (direct-vent appliances excepted)

Temperature

The city says it's universally recommended to keep water at tubs and showers no hotter than 125°F to prevent scalding. Most dishwashers run at about 140°F.

Tank or Tankless?

  • Tankless: the DOE says these last about 20 years and can be 8% to 34% more efficient, but output runs about 2 to 5 gallons per minute and the up-front cost is higher
  • Storage tank: a lower purchase price and a 10- to 15-year life
  • Payback: Scottsdale's guide notes modern high-efficiency heaters "often can pay for themselves in energy savings within 3-5 years"

Hard water affects tankless units too, so ask your plumber about descaling.

FAQ: Water Heaters in Arizona

How long does a water heater last in Arizona?

About 12 years on average; 10 to 15 for tanks, about 20 for tankless.

How often should I flush my water heater with hard water?

Drain at least 5 gallons every six months.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Scottsdale?

Yes, a Minimum Plumbing Permit.

Is a tankless water heater worth it in Arizona?

It depends on your household's flow needs and budget.
