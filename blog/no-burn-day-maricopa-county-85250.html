No Burn Day in Maricopa County: Fireplace, Fire Pit and Chimenea Rules for Scottsdale Homes

What does a No Burn Day mean in Maricopa County? No wood fires in fireplaces, fire pits or chimeneas; gas is allowed. Here's how to check, and the fines, for 85250.

What does a No Burn Day mean? On a No Burn Day, Maricopa County allows only gaseous fuels, including gas logs, in a fireplace, woodstove, chimenea or outdoor fire pit. No wood. The county calls these restrictions when it forecasts high levels of fine particle pollution, and they last 24 hours, starting at midnight.

How do you check? Use the county's Clean Air mobile app, sign up for email or text alerts at Maricopa.gov/AQ, follow @MaricopaAir, or call 602-506-6400. On any other day in burn season, you can burn gaseous fuels or clean, dry, seasoned wood.

Data note: Rules and fines come from the Maricopa County Air Quality Department's burning page (reviewed September 2026), its policy P-26 on residential burning restrictions and its No Burn Day fact sheet. No market statistics are included in this post.

No Burn Day Rules at a Glance

  • Allowed on a No Burn Day: natural gas, propane and gas logs
  • Not allowed: wood fires in fireplaces, woodstoves, chimeneas and outdoor fire pits
  • How long: 24 hours, midnight to midnight
  • Why: a forecast of high PM10 or PM2.5 (particle pollution)
  • Penalty: a warning first, then $50, $100 and $250 under policy P-26
  • Check: Clean Air app, 602-506-6400, or alerts at Maricopa.gov/AQ

What You Can Burn, and When

On a No Burn Day

Only gaseous fuels. That means a gas fireplace, a gas fire table, or gas logs in a wood-burning fireplace. Everything else stays dark until midnight.

On a Normal Burn-Season Day

You can burn gaseous fuels or clean, dry, seasoned wood. The county defines seasoned wood as having 20% moisture or less, and that category includes charcoal, pellets and manufactured firelogs.

Never

Policy P-26's prohibited list includes garbage, grass clippings, green plants, furniture and chemically treated wood, and the county's burning page says yard waste such as tree trimmings can't be burned either. Your winter pruning goes in the green bin, not the fire pit.

May 1 to September 30

In the county's Area A, which covers metro Phoenix and Scottsdale, fires for warmth or recreation are prohibited in this window, including wood chimeneas and wood fire pits. Cooking fires that meet the county's rules, including a fire no larger than 2 square feet, are a separate category.

How Maricopa County Decides

A No Burn Day is a forecast-based call. Under P-26, the county restricts residential burning when it forecasts pollution above health-based thresholds, including PM10 at 120 µg/m³ or PM2.5 at 30 µg/m³ over 24 hours. Winter is the usual season for them because still, cool nights trap smoke close to the ground.

That matters in an established neighborhood like 85250, where homes sit close together and many have wood-burning fireplaces. One smoky fire on a calm night lingers on the whole street.

Fines and Exemptions

  • First violation: usually a warning
  • Second: $50
  • Third: $100
  • Fourth and later: $250
  • Rule 314 minimum penalty: $200, per the county's burning page

The county lists partial exemptions, such as a home where the device is the sole source of heat. Approved devices may still be used if they produce no visible emissions after the first 20 minutes. Check the county's page for the details before relying on one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a No Burn Day in Maricopa County?

A day the county restricts burning because of a high pollution forecast. Only gas may be burned.

How do I know if today is a No Burn Day?

The Clean Air mobile app, alerts at Maricopa.gov/AQ, or 602-506-6400.

Can I use my gas fire pit on a No Burn Day?

Yes. Gaseous fuels, including gas logs, are allowed.

What is the fine for burning on a No Burn Day?

A warning first, then $50, $100 and $250 under policy P-26.
