When You Buy a House in Arizona, Is Everything Left in It Yours? A Buyer's Guide for 85250

Is everything a seller leaves behind yours? What Arizona's standard contract says conveys, the seller's cleanout duty and what to do with leftovers in 85250.

Is everything left in the house yours after closing? Not automatically. Under the Arizona REALTORS® Residential Resale Real Estate Purchase Contract (current version updated February 2026), you buy the home's fixtures, meaning property attached or affixed to it, plus any personal property checked in the contract. The seller's remaining belongings are still the seller's.

And the seller has to take them. The same contract requires that by the earlier of possession or close of escrow, "all personal property not included in the sale and debris will be removed." Your final walkthrough is where you confirm it happened. If it didn't, document it and work through your agent before you toss anything.

What Conveys Under Arizona's Standard Contract

Fixtures: Attached Means It Stays

The contract conveys all existing fixtures and the means to operate them, such as remote controls. Its list includes:

  • Built-in appliances, ceiling fans and remotes
  • Free-standing range/oven
  • Light fixtures, draperies and other window coverings, and towel, curtain and drapery rods
  • Wall-mounted TV brackets and hardware (the TVs themselves are excluded)
  • Garage door openers and remotes, storage sheds, water-misting systems
  • Outdoor landscaping: shrubbery, trees and unpotted plants
  • Smart home devices, with access transferred

Included Only If the Seller Owns Them

  • Solar and other affixed alternate power systems
  • In-ground pool and spa equipment and covers
  • Security and fire systems, water purification systems, water softeners

Personal Property: Only If Checked

  • Refrigerator, washer, dryer, above-ground spa/hot tub and "other" lines
  • Transferred with no monetary value, free and clear of liens

Leased Items Don't Convey

The seller must give notice of leased items within three days of acceptance. You can disapprove within the Inspection Period or five days after notice, whichever is later.

The Seller's Cleanout Duty and Your Walkthrough

  • The cleanout: personal property not included and debris must be gone by the earlier of possession or COE
  • The walkthrough: you may confirm agreed repairs are done and the home is in substantially the same condition as at acceptance
  • The catch: skip it, and you release the seller and brokers from liability for defects you could have discovered
  • AAR's own advice to buyers: "If the property is unacceptable, speak up. After the closing may be too late."

In Chaparral Park's established ranch homes, look past the living spaces: garage cabinets, side yards, attic access, backyard sheds and the pool equipment pad are where leftovers tend to hide.

Items Left After Closing: A Measured Approach

Document First

  • Date-stamped photos of every item and location
  • Written notice from your agent to the seller's agent, with a reasonable pickup deadline

Hold the Valuables

  • Documents, heirlooms, firearms, vehicles and anything titled call for an attorney's input before you move them
  • Junk is different from belongings. Debris is plainly covered by the cleanout clause; a dining set may not be

Know the Contract's Process

The AAR contract routes buyer-seller disputes to mediation, then arbitration unless a party opts out, with small claims (up to $5,000) excluded. Whether you can recover removal costs is a legal question for your attorney.

Arizona's Abandoned-Property Law: What It Does and Doesn't Cover

The statute most people find is A.R.S. 33-1370, and it's part of Arizona's residential landlord-tenant law:

  • Abandonment generally means a tenant absent seven days with rent unpaid ten days, or absent five days with rent unpaid five days and no belongings left
  • The landlord posts and mails notice, then may retake the unit five days later
  • Remaining belongings must be held 14 calendar days before donation or sale

It does not govern a home sale. A seller who leaves things behind isn't a tenant, so those timelines shouldn't be borrowed. Your purchase contract and an attorney's read on your facts are the right guides.

Scottsdale Disposal and Donation Options

  • Brush and bulk: Chaparral Park is in Area 4 (south of Indian Bend Road); remaining 2026 weeks are Nov. 16–20 and Dec. 21–25. See our Scottsdale bulk trash schedule for pile rules
  • Appliance collection: the last Thursday of each month, by request; refrigerators and freezers need the Freon recovered by a certified technician first, and doors removed
  • Move-in boxes: a dedicated pickup for new residents with city solid waste accounts; empty and flatten them
  • Not drop-off sites: city facilities don't accept appliances, electronics, moving boxes or hazardous waste
  • Reuse: the Scottsdale Public Library accepts gently used materials in good condition; local charities such as St. Vincent de Paul offer scheduled pickups
  • Solid Waste: 480-312-5600

FAQ: Items Left Behind After You Buy

When you buy a house in Arizona, is everything left in it yours?

No. Fixtures and checked personal property convey; the seller must remove the rest by possession or COE.

How long does someone's stuff stay on your property after closing?

The standard contract has no grace period. A post-possession agreement, if any, controls.

How long before property is considered abandoned in Arizona?

A.R.S. 33-1370 sets rental timelines, but it covers landlords and tenants, not home sales.

What can I do with items a seller left behind in Scottsdale?

Use brush and bulk, appliance collection or donation, once ownership is confirmed in writing.
