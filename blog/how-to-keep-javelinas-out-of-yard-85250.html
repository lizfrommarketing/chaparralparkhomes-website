How to Keep Javelinas Out of Your Yard in Scottsdale: What Arizona Game and Fish Recommends

How do you keep javelinas out of your yard? Remove food and water, fence with a 4-foot wall and buried footer, and never feed them. AZGFD's advice for 85250 homes.

How do you keep javelinas out of your yard? Take away what they came for. Arizona Game and Fish (AZGFD) says javelina are drawn by food, water and shelter, so feed pets indoors, keep birdseed off the ground, pick up fallen fruit and put water out of reach. For a lasting fix, AZGFD suggests a 4-foot block wall or chain-link fence with a concrete footer buried 8 to 12 inches.

What should you never do? Feed them. AZGFD says bites are almost always tied to feeding, and in Maricopa County, feeding or attracting wildlife is a petty offense under A.R.S. 13-2927.

Data note: Behavior and prevention advice comes from the Arizona Game and Fish Department's "Living with Javelina" guidance. The feeding law is A.R.S. 13-2927. No market statistics are included in this post.

Javelina Facts at a Glance

  • Not pigs: javelina are peccaries, a separate family
  • Size: about 40 to 60 pounds, roughly 19 inches tall
  • Herds: 2 to more than 20 animals
  • When: mostly at night, but often out by day in cold weather
  • Babies: born year-round, most often November through March
  • Senses: very poor eyesight, a keen sense of smell
  • Travel routes: washes and dense vegetation

Step 1: Remove the Attractants

Food

  • Feed pets inside, or pick up bowls right away
  • Keep birdseed off the ground and skip quail blocks
  • Pick up fallen fruit and nuts from citrus and other trees
  • Store pet food and feed indoors

Trash

  • Use cans with locking lids
  • Put cans out the morning of pickup, not the night before
  • Rinse cans with a bleach solution to cut odors

Water and Shelter

  • Keep water above their reach or behind fencing; they'll chew drip lines and hoses
  • Block access under porches, decks and crawl spaces

Plants

AZGFD lists cacti, succulents, bulbs, tubers and fruit- and nut-dropping trees among their favorites. Protect new plantings, especially in winter.

Step 2: Fence Them Out

  • Block wall or chain link: at least 4 feet high
  • Footer: concrete, buried 8 to 12 inches, because javelina dig
  • Electric option: a single strand 8 to 10 inches above the ground around gardens; check local ordinances first
  • Gates: keep them closed, especially at night
  • Gardens: fence vegetable beds and new plantings separately if the whole yard isn't enclosed

Many 85250 yards already have block walls, so the job is often patching rather than building. The weak points are usually gaps under gates, side-yard openings and spots where the soil has washed out under the wall.

Step 3: Haze Them, Safely

If javelina show up anyway, AZGFD suggests making the yard unpleasant:

  • Loud noises or small rocks tossed toward them, not at them
  • A blast from the hose
  • Vinegar, or ammonia diluted 1:9 with water, in a squirt gun; keep it out of their eyes and away from wetlands

Keep dogs leashed and away from the herd. Javelina get defensive when cornered, protecting young, or when they hear or smell a dog.

A 10-Minute Yard Check

  • Walk the wall line and look for dig marks or washed-out soil
  • Check that gates close fully with no gap at the bottom
  • Look under citrus for dropped fruit
  • Move pet bowls and birdseed inside or up high
  • Check drip lines and hose bibs for chew marks

When to Call for Help

If one is trapped in your yard, open a gate and leave. If it's still there the next day, call a wildlife control company or AZGFD. The after-hours dispatcher is 623-236-7201.

Don't trap or harm them. Javelina are a big game species; AZGFD says it's unlawful to injure, kill or trap them, and removal usually results in the animal's death.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I keep javelinas out of my yard?

Remove food and water, then fence with a 4-foot wall and a buried footer.

What scares javelinas away?

Noise, a hose, or diluted vinegar or ammonia, per AZGFD.

Is it illegal to feed javelinas in Arizona?

In Maricopa County, yes: a petty offense under A.R.S. 13-2927.

Are javelinas pigs?

No. They're peccaries.
