Is Scottsdale Running Out of Water? What the 2026 Colorado River Cuts Mean for 85250

Is Scottsdale running out of water? No. There are no mandatory restrictions, but a possible 17% Colorado River cut in 2027 and a Nov. 1 rate increase matter for 85250 owners.

Is Scottsdale running out of water? No. The City of Scottsdale says the 2026 Tier 1 Colorado River shortage does not reduce its supply, and there are no mandatory water restrictions. Taps in 85250 work exactly as they did last year.

So why is everyone asking? Because the rules that divide the Colorado River expire at the end of 2026. Axios reported on Oct. 22 that Scottsdale's river allocation could fall 17% in 2027, the price of a planned water purification plant has climbed to as much as $164 million, and a 4.5% water rate increase takes effect Nov. 1.

Data note: Figures come from the City of Scottsdale's Colorado River Update and from reporting by Axios Phoenix (Oct. 22, 2026) and KJZZ (June 2, 2026). Future Colorado River allocations are not final.

Where Scottsdale's Water Comes From

The city runs a diversified portfolio, which is why a shortage on one river doesn't turn off the tap:

  • Colorado River via the Central Arizona Project (CAP): about 70% of supply, per KJZZ
  • Salt and Verde rivers, delivered through SRP
  • Reclaimed water, treated and put back to use
  • Groundwater, plus water the city has stored underground for future use

What Changed in 2026

The Colorado River Rules Are Expiring

  • The 2007 interim guidelines that govern shortages expire at the end of 2026
  • The U.S. Bureau of Reclamation released its Final Environmental Impact Statement in July 2026
  • Lake Mead stood near 1,039 feet in August 2026, per the City of Scottsdale

A Possible 17% Cut in 2027

  • Axios reported Scottsdale's CAP allocation could drop 17% in 2027
  • The city has not been using its full allocation, which gives it some cushion
  • No mandatory restrictions have been announced

The Price of New Water Is Rising

  • Axios: the proposed advanced water purification plant, which would recycle wastewater into drinking water, is estimated at up to $164 million
  • Adding a South Scottsdale wastewater pump station could push the total to as much as $261 million
  • Two related projects totaling about $87 million, including a wastewater pump station near Indian Bend and Hayden roads, were submitted for the FY2027-28 capital budget
  • City Council took no action at the October briefing

Why It Matters in 85250

Your Water Bill

  • Nov. 1, 2026: a 4.5% water rate increase takes effect (KJZZ)
  • Part of the new revenue is earmarked for additional water sources, such as Harquahala Valley groundwater and a possible Bartlett Dam expansion
  • Axios quoted a city official telling council that "water rates may go up" again as projects move forward

Your Neighborhood

  • The Indian Bend and Hayden pump station project sits right on the edge of 85250, near Chaparral Park
  • Large capital projects can mean construction and lane closures; watch city notices if the project is funded

Your Yard

  • Stage 1 drought status (since 2021) means conservation is encouraged, not required
  • If you are thinking about a switch to desert landscaping, the city offers rebates. See our sister site's Scottsdale water rebates guide and our xeriscape garden post

Does Water Affect Home Values?

There's no data showing a water discount on 85250 homes, and I won't invent one. What water does affect is ownership cost and buyer questions:

  • Monthly bills for homes with large lawns or pools
  • Landscape choices: low-water yards are easier to defend to buyers asking about the river
  • Disclosure: Arizona sellers already complete the SPDS, which asks about water source and service. See our seller disclosure guide

Practical Steps for 85250 Owners

  • Audit your irrigation before winter: shorter, less frequent cycles once temperatures drop
  • Check for leaks with your meter: turn off all water and watch for movement
  • Consider partial turf removal; front-yard grass is often the easiest cut
  • Read the city's Colorado River Update for official changes; Scottsdale Water Resources is at 480-312-5650

FAQ: Scottsdale Water Supply

Is Scottsdale running out of water?

No. The 2026 Tier 1 shortage does not reduce Scottsdale's supply, and there are no mandatory restrictions.

Is there a water shortage in Scottsdale right now?

There's a Tier 1 shortage on the Colorado River, but Scottsdale remains in voluntary Stage 1 conservation.

Are Scottsdale water rates going up?

Yes, 4.5% on Nov. 1, 2026, with more increases possible as new projects are funded.

How much of Scottsdale's water comes from the Colorado River?

About 70%, and that allocation could fall 17% in 2027.
