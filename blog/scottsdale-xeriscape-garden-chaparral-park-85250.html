Scottsdale Xeriscape Garden at Chaparral Park: Desert Landscaping Ideas for 85250 Yards

The Scottsdale Xeriscape Garden at Chaparral Park shows 7,000+ desert plants on six acres. How to visit, what xeriscape means and fall planting tips for 85250 yards.

Where can you see desert landscaping ideas in Scottsdale? Right in the middle of 85250. The Scottsdale Xeriscape Garden at Chaparral Park is a six-acre demonstration garden with more than 7,000 Arizona-friendly plants and about 200 species, set against Camelback Mountain. It's free, open from sunrise to 10:30 p.m., and easy to miss from the street.

Why visit before you redo your yard? Because you can see full-grown desert plants side by side, read the city's signage on plant selection, water harvesting and care, and then plant at the right time. University of Arizona (UA) Cooperative Extension calls late fall the ideal planting window in the desert, which makes the next few weeks prime time.

Data note: Garden details come from the City of Scottsdale's Xeriscape Garden and Parks & Trees pages; planting and irrigation guidance from UA Cooperative Extension publication AZ1048, "Care of Desert-Adapted Plants," and AMWUA's "Landscaping with Style" guide; rebate terms from Scottsdale Water's current single-family grass removal application (checked Oct. 22, 2026).

How to Find the Garden

  • Address: 8111 E. McDonald Drive, Scottsdale, AZ 85250, inside Chaparral Park
  • Parking: off Hayden Road, just south of McDonald, next to the dog park
  • Walk in: paths start beside the dog park and between the softball fields behind the restrooms
  • Hours: sunrise to 10:30 p.m.
  • Questions: the Master Gardener hotline listed by the City is 602-827-8201

What You'll See

  • Self-guided walking tour with signage on selecting and installing Arizona-friendly plants and trees, water harvesting and plant care
  • An interactive plant guide, per the City's Parks & Trees page
  • A National Wildlife Federation Certified Wildlife Habitat
  • A buried water reservoir from the adjacent Chaparral Water Treatment Plant, hidden beneath the plantings

What Xeriscape Actually Means

UA Cooperative Extension traces the word to the Greek xeros, "dry." A xeriscape is a water-conserving landscape designed to fit the existing environment, using native and other drought-resistant plants that tolerate low humidity, low rainfall and alkaline soils.

It does not mean gravel and nothing else. AMWUA's guide notes that small, well-planned grass areas can fit into a xeriscape, especially for children or pets.

Five Ideas to Borrow From the Garden

  • Group plants by thirst. AMWUA recommends putting plants with similar water needs on the same irrigation valve
  • Plan for mature size. UA advises considering a plant's size at maturity before choosing its spot, and keeping large trees away from overhead utility lines
  • Mulch the soil. Bark, wood chips, decomposed granite or crushed rock reduce evaporation and weeds
  • Use drip. UA recommends drip, a soaker hose or a slow trickle so water doesn't run off faster than soil absorbs it
  • Harvest rainwater. It's one of the garden's own signage topics

How to Plant Desert Plants This Fall

UA's planting steps, condensed:

  • Timing: late fall is ideal, except for frost-sensitive species; late spring is the runner-up
  • Hole: three to five times as wide as the root ball and no deeper than its height
  • Drainage test: fill the hole with water first; it should drain within 12 to 24 hours
  • Backfill: native soil; keep the top of the root ball level with or slightly above grade, because planting too deep can cause crown rot
  • Fertilizer: not required at planting

Watering New Plants

  • Establishment: supplemental water for at least one growing season; plants are typically established in about two to three years
  • Deep, not frequent: light, frequent watering builds shallow, weak roots; deep, less frequent watering builds drought tolerance
  • Go wide: mature root zones can spread 1.5 to 4 times the width of a tree's canopy, so add emitters outward as plants grow

Removing Grass? Mind the October 31 Deadline

Scottsdale Water's single-family grass removal rebate pays $2 per square foot, up to $5,000. The current application window closes October 31, 2026, and you need a pre-inspection and Notice to Proceed before you remove any grass. The 500-square-foot minimum is waived if you remove all remaining grass. Full details are in our sister site's Scottsdale water rebates guide.

FAQ: The Xeriscape Garden and Desert Landscaping

Where is the Scottsdale Xeriscape Garden?

Inside Chaparral Park, 8111 E. McDonald Dr. Park off Hayden, just south of McDonald, by the dog park.

What is xeriscape landscaping?

A water-conserving landscape suited to the local environment, built on native and drought-resistant plants (UA Cooperative Extension).

When is the best time to plant desert landscaping in Phoenix and Scottsdale?

Late fall, per the UA, while soil is warm and air is cool.

Does Scottsdale pay you to remove grass?

Yes: $2 per sq. ft., up to $5,000. Apply by October 31, 2026, and get your Notice to Proceed first.
