When to Plant Wildflower Seeds in Arizona: Sow Now for Spring Color in Scottsdale and 85250 Yards

In the low desert, sow spring wildflowers like poppies, desert marigold and bluebells in fall, ideally by early November. How to plant, water and protect seeds in 85250.

When should you plant wildflower seeds in Arizona? In the low desert, sow spring-blooming wildflowers in the fall, and the sooner in November the better. University of Arizona Extension's November guide says to sow spring wildflowers such as California and Mexican poppies, desert bluebells and desert marigold early in the month, and to keep the area moist until seeds germinate.

Why now is a good moment: the storm that ran from Friday, Oct. 30, through Sunday, Nov. 1 left 4.25 inches of rain in Scottsdale, according to totals reported by KTAR. Native wildflowers follow the desert's rainy seasons, so the next few weeks are your window for a spring show.

Arizona Wildflower Timing at a Glance

  • Spring bloomers: plant in fall; the Arizona Native Plant Society ties bloom to the winter/spring rainy season
  • Summer (monsoon) bloomers: plant in spring
  • Best November timing: early in the month, per UA Extension's monthly reminders
  • Frost: UA notes the first frost sometimes arrives mid-to-late November, so get seed down soon
  • Bloom season: cool-season flowers planted in fall bloom through May in the low desert, per UA's flower planting guide

What to Plant for Spring Color

UA Extension and the Arizona Native Plant Society both list desert natives that do well from seed:

  • California and Mexican poppies for orange and gold carpets
  • Desert bluebells and desert marigold
  • Tidy tips, cowpen daisy and white chicory
  • Lupines, chia, globemallow and Parry's penstemon, a tall bloomer the Arizona Native Plant Society says attracts hummingbirds

Shop carefully. UA Extension warns that inexpensive seed mixes may not suit our climate and can even contain prohibited or invasive species. It also flags cosmos, bachelor buttons and sweet alyssum as common fillers.

How to Plant Wildflower Seeds in an 85250 Yard

Pick the spot

  • Full sun, with good drainage; avoid compacted or poorly drained soil
  • Gravel or decomposed granite works well, per the Arizona Native Plant Society: scatter seed, then hose it down into the rock for seed-to-soil contact and protection from birds

Prepare the soil

  • Pull weeds first; UA Extension says to pull annual weeds before they go to seed
  • On bare or compacted soil, loosen just the top inch with a hard rake
  • Skip nitrogen fertilizer; UA Extension says it can be detrimental to wildflowers

Sow lightly

  • Broadcast the seed and rake lightly
  • Bury it no deeper than 1/8 inch; some seed will remain visible, and that's fine, since seeds need light as well as moisture

Water wisely

  • Keep the soil moist, not soggy, for up to three weeks or until seedlings emerge
  • Once seedlings are 1-2 inches tall, water only when you see wilting or yellowing
  • If winter rain arrives, let it do the work; adjust your timer

Protect the seedlings

  • A temporary low fence can keep rabbits out, per UA Extension
  • Keep frost cloth handy for cold snaps; see our frost protection guide

Seeing It in Person

Chaparral Park's Xeriscape Garden is a good place to see desert plants in a real landscape before you choose. If you'd rather collect seed than buy it, the Arizona Native Plant Society notes that gathering seed in state or national parks is prohibited and other public lands require a permit. Your own yard, or a neighbor's with permission, is fair game.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should you plant wildflower seeds in Arizona?

Plant spring bloomers in fall and monsoon bloomers in spring. In November, sow early.

Is it too late to plant wildflower seeds in Phoenix in November?

Not if you plant soon. UA lists early November for poppies, bluebells and desert marigold.

How deep do you plant wildflower seeds?

No deeper than 1/8 inch. Some seeds should stay visible.

How often should you water wildflower seeds in Arizona?

Keep soil moist for up to three weeks, then water only when seedlings look stressed.
