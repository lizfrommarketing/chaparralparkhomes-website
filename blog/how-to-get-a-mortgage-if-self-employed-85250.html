How to Get a Mortgage If You're Self-Employed: Tax Returns, the Two-Year Rule and What 85250 Buyers Should Prepare

How to get a mortgage if you're self-employed: Fannie Mae's two-year history rule, when one year of tax returns can work, and the documents 85250 buyers should gather first.

How do you get a mortgage if you're self-employed? Plan on showing two years of signed federal tax returns, personal and sometimes business, so the lender can prove your income is stable and likely to continue. That's the general rule in Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, which sets the standards for conventional loans sold to Fannie Mae.

There are exceptions in both directions. With a full year of returns from your current business and a track record in the same field, less than two years can work. And if your business has existed for five years with you owning 25% or more, one year of returns may be enough. The catch for many owners: lenders qualify you on the income your returns show after deductions.

Data note: Underwriting rules are from Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, sections B3-3.2-01 (self-employed borrowers) and related guidance, published Nov. 4, 2026. Rates are from Freddie Mac's PMMS, Nov. 25, 2026. Read November 27, 2026. Lenders may add their own requirements; FHA, VA and non-conventional loans have different rules.

Who Counts as Self-Employed?

Fannie Mae's definition is simple: anyone with a 25% or greater ownership interest in a business is treated as self-employed. That includes:

  • Sole proprietors and independent contractors filing a Schedule C
  • Partners in a partnership
  • Owners of an S corporation or corporation with a 25%+ stake

Own less than 25%? Your income is generally reviewed differently, often through the K-1. Ask your lender.

The Two-Year Rule, and Its Exceptions

The standard

Fannie Mae generally requires a two-year history of prior earnings to show the income is likely to continue.

Less than two years

Income from less than two years of self-employment may be considered if: - Your most recent signed personal and business tax returns show a full 12 months of income from the current business, and - You have documented prior income at the same or greater level in a field with the same products or services, or in an occupation with similar responsibilities

Lenders must weigh your experience and the debt your business has taken on.

When one year of returns can work

Fannie Mae lets lenders accept one year of personal and business returns if: - The business has existed for five years, and - You've held 25% or more ownership for five consecutive years

When business returns may be waived

With two years of signed personal returns, the lender may skip business returns if you've been self-employed in the same business for five years and your self-employment income increased over the past two years.

How Lenders Read Your Income

  • Written analysis: the lender must prepare a written evaluation of your personal and business income to determine stable, continuing income
  • Distributions matter: Fannie Mae notes that partnership or S corporation income reported on your Form 1040 may not equal what was actually distributed to you
  • The business itself: lenders look at its stability, location, demand for what you sell, financial strength and ability to keep paying you
  • Transcripts: IRS-issued transcripts of your returns can substitute for copies, if complete and legible

Using Business Money for the Down Payment

If you qualify with self-employment income and want to use business funds for the down payment, closing costs or reserves, Fannie Mae requires a business cash flow analysis to confirm the withdrawal won't hurt the business. Expect requests for several months of business account statements or a current balance sheet.

Tip: moving money from business to personal accounts well before you apply makes the paper trail simpler. Talk to your CPA and lender first.

Your Pre-Approval Checklist

  • Two years of signed personal federal tax returns, all schedules
  • Two years of business returns, if applicable (1065, 1120S, 1120)
  • Year-to-date profit and loss statement, if your lender asks
  • Recent business and personal bank statements
  • Proof of ownership share, such as articles of incorporation, a business license or an IRS EIN confirmation letter
  • A list of business debts

Frequently Asked Questions

How many years of tax returns do you need for a mortgage if you're self-employed?

Usually two. One can work after five years of 25%+ ownership.

Can you get a mortgage with less than two years of self-employment?

Sometimes, with 12 months of returns and a history in the same field.

Who counts as self-employed for a mortgage?

Anyone with a 25% or greater ownership interest in a business.

Can I use business funds for my down payment?

Possibly, after the lender's business cash flow analysis.
