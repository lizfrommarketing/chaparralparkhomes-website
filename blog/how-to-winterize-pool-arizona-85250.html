How to Winterize a Pool in Arizona: November Checklist for Scottsdale 85250 Homes

How do you winterize a pool in Arizona? Most Valley pools stay open. Balance chemistry, keep circulation, add a cover and plan for rare freezes.

How do you winterize a pool in Arizona? For most Scottsdale and 85250 backyards, winterizing means a lighter season, not a northern-style shutdown. Pentair's Winter Pool Care: Sun Belt Edition says desert Southwest pools typically stay open all year and do not need a full close unless a cold front or winter storm could damage equipment or chemistry.

What changes in November? You still test and balance the water, keep water circulating, pull leaves and dust, decide on a cover, and make sure the system can protect itself on a rare freeze night. You do not invent chemical dosages from a blog — you follow the CDC ranges and your product labels.

Arizona Winter Pool Care at a Glance

  • Default approach in the Sun Belt: keep the pool open (Pentair)
  • Full close: mainly when a cold front or winter storm threatens equipment
  • Chemistry floor (CDC): pH 7.0–7.8; free chlorine at least 1 ppm ( at least 2 ppm if using cyanuric acid / dichlor / trichlor)
  • Partial-closure testing cadence (Pentair): every two to three weeks if you dial equipment back
  • Cover benefit: less debris; less evaporation of water and chlorine (Pentair); Scottsdale Water also pushes covers for water savings
  • January evaporation estimate (Scottsdale Water): about 2.6 inches / 653 gallons for a 400 sq. ft. pool
  • Scottsdale December mean: 53.6°F; January mean 54.9°F (ASU Climate Office / NOAA normals)

Step 1: Decide Open, Partial or Storm Close

Pentair frames Sun Belt choices clearly:

Stay open (most 85250 pools)

If you are in a desert Southwest climate, keep monitoring chemistry and enjoy the pool on warm afternoons. Scottsdale's winter daytime highs average in the mid-60s, so many households swim or at least keep the water blue for guests.

Partial close

Pentair suggests considering a partial or total closure when average winter temperatures hover around 45°F. Scottsdale's monthly winter means are warmer than that, so a true partial close is a household choice, not a climate requirement. If you do ease off:

  1. Do not lower the water level below the skimmers — circulation still matters
  2. Keep equipment able to run
  3. Balance chemistry and retest every two to three weeks
  4. Keep cleaning leaves and dust
  5. Add a cover
  6. Remove gear you do not need in frost risk, such as some robotic cleaners or booster pumps

Storm / freeze plan

Pentair also says to monitor weather and have a freeze-protection plan. Running the pump during a hard freeze reduces the chance of water freezing in plumbing. Automation with freeze protection can help, but only when power and sensors work.

Step 2: Balance Chemistry the Responsible Way

CDC Healthy Swimming is the public-health baseline for home pools:

  • Keep pH between 7.0 and 7.8
  • Keep free chlorine at least 1 ppm in pools
  • If you use cyanuric acid or stabilized chlorine (dichlor / trichlor), keep free chlorine at least 2 ppm
  • Test with a DPD kit when you can; strips are less accurate
  • Follow the manufacturer directions on every bottle — never mix chemicals

This post will not invent target alkalinity dosages, shock amounts or algaecide recipes. Arizona winters still grow algae on warm stretches, so a local pool professional or your product's winter kit instructions are the right place for product-specific amounts.

Step 3: Clean, Cover and Conserve Water

November in Chaparral Park means leaf drop, dust and the start of lower evaporation — but evaporation never stops.

Scottsdale Water's homeowner conservation page recommends:

  • Using a pool cover to reduce evaporation and help keep the pool cleaner
  • Checking equipment for leaks (wet spots, mineral crust on pipes)
  • Running a bucket test if you suspect a hidden leak
  • Avoiding unnecessary draining when chemistry can be corrected instead

Pentair adds that a cover also slows chlorine loss. For many 85250 homes, a solid or mesh safety cover is as much about debris and evaporation as temperature.

Above-ground pools follow the same Sun Belt logic: stay open unless a storm threatens, keep circulation through the skimmer and protect exposed plumbing on freeze nights.

Step 4: Equipment and Safety Checks

  • Confirm the pump, filter and timer still circulate enough water
  • Clear skimmer and pump baskets more often during leaf drop
  • Verify freeze-protection on variable-speed pumps or automation
  • Keep barriers and gates working — see Scottsdale pool fence requirements
  • Store chemicals locked, cool and dry

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you winterize a pool in Arizona?

Keep it open in most cases. Balance chemistry to CDC ranges, maintain circulation, clean debris, consider a cover and set freeze protection for rare cold nights.

Do I need to close my pool for winter in Scottsdale?

Usually no. Pentair says Sun Belt desert pools typically stay open unless a cold front threatens equipment.

What chlorine and pH should I keep in winter?

CDC: pH 7.0–7.8, free chlorine at least 1 ppm ( at least 2 ppm with cyanuric acid). Follow product labels for amounts.

Should I cover my pool in Arizona winter?

Yes, a cover helps with debris and evaporation. Scottsdale Water cites about 2.6 inches of January evaporative loss for a 400 sq. ft. pool.
