Should You Get a Pre-Listing Inspection Before Selling Your Scottsdale 85250 Home?

Is a pre-listing inspection worth it in Arizona? It can cut surprises in the buyer's 10-day inspection period, but what it finds you must disclose. Here's how to decide.

Is a pre-listing inspection worth it? For many Chaparral Park sellers, yes, especially in homes built decades ago, where roofs, pipes and HVAC systems have aged. A pre-listing inspection shows you what a buyer's inspector will likely find before you set your price, so repairs and negotiations happen on your timeline instead of inside the buyer's 10-day inspection period.

What's the catch? Disclosure. Arizona law requires sellers to disclose known material facts. Once a report tells you the water heater leaks or the roof needs work, that's something you know, and it belongs on your Seller's Property Disclosure Statement (SPDS).

Pre-Listing Inspection at a Glance

  • Who orders it: the seller, before listing
  • Who does it: an Arizona certified home inspector, verifiable through the Board of Technical Registration
  • Standard used: the Arizona Chapter of ASHI's Standards of Professional Practice (adopted Oct. 27, 2023)
  • Pools and spas: not required in a standard inspection; ask for it separately
  • Disclosure: material findings belong on your SPDS
  • Buyer's rights: buyers still get their own inspection period, 10 days by default

How It Fits Into an Arizona Sale

The Buyer's Clock

Under the Arizona REALTORS resale contract, the buyer's inspection period is 10 days unless the parties agree otherwise. In that window the buyer inspects, then can ask for repairs or cancel. The seller must deliver the SPDS within 3 days of acceptance.

Why Timing Matters

Ten days is a short window to get roofer bids, plumber quotes and a counteroffer ready. Sellers who already know their home's condition can answer repair requests in hours, not days, and keep the escrow calm.

What the Seller Warrants

The contract's seller warranties say the seller has disclosed all material latent defects and information known to the seller that would materially and adversely affect the price. The SPDS advisory adds that these disclosures are warranties that survive closing.

Pros and Cons

Reasons to Get One

  • Fewer surprises once you're under contract
  • Time to fix items with your own contractors and bids
  • Pricing confidence, because you know what the home needs
  • Cleaner disclosure, with clear answers on the SPDS

Reasons Some Sellers Skip It

  • Whatever it finds becomes known, and must be disclosed
  • Buyers will still hire their own inspector
  • A newer or recently updated home may gain less from one
  • Some issues, such as termites, are inspected separately; the contract has the buyer order and pay for that inspection

Choosing an Inspector

Arizona's rules for certified home inspectors, R4-30-301.01, are built to keep the inspection independent. An inspector can't pay referral fees to real estate agents, can't profit from repairs on a home they inspected for 24 months, and can't inspect a home they have a financial interest in selling as a real estate licensee.

  • Verify the certification with the Board of Technical Registration's search
  • Ask whether the pool and spa are included
  • Ask how photos and the summary page are delivered

After the Report

Sort findings into three piles: fix before listing, disclose and price in, and leave for the buyer to weigh. For help with the disclosure form itself, see our guide to the Arizona SPDS. If you'd rather not make repairs at all, read should I sell my house as-is first.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a pre-listing inspection?

A seller-ordered home inspection, done before listing, to the same Arizona standards.

Should I get a pre-listing inspection?

Often yes for older homes or known issues, as long as you're ready to disclose what it finds.

Do I have to disclose a pre-listing inspection report in Arizona?

You must disclose known material facts. Ask an attorney about sharing the report itself.

Does a pre-listing inspection replace the buyer's inspection?

No. Buyers get their own inspection period, 10 days by default.
